brAIn writes the automation you want on a bad night: what the house should do when smoke,
a leak or a freeze is detected. It lists every device the automation would touch, and you
decide.

<Aside type="caution" title="What a playbook never does">
- **brAIn never runs a playbook.** It writes one. Home Assistant runs it, and only after
  you press **Apply**. No setting turns one on.
- **No playbook unlocks a door or disarms an alarm.** Locks and alarm panels are refused in
  every playbook, and the finished automation is checked for them. A smoke detector is the
  sensor most likely to go off over burnt toast, and a false alarm at 3am must not open the
  house.
- **Garage doors and windows are not opened.** Only blinds, shades and curtains.
- **The freeze playbook turns no boiler on.** It tells you, and nothing else.
</Aside>

## The three playbooks

A playbook is offered only when your house has the sensor for it. Many houses get none.

| Playbook | Runs when | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Smoke or carbon monoxide** | Any `smoke`, `carbon_monoxide` or `gas` binary sensor turns on | Every light to full brightness. Heating and cooling off. Blinds, shades and curtains open. Then it tells you, naming the room. |
| **Water leak** | Any `moisture` binary sensor turns on | Water valves closed. Water shutoff switches thrown to closed. Water heaters off. Then it tells you, naming the room. |
| **Freezing with the heating stopped** | The coldest room brAIn has [measured](/brain/climate/) is below 5 °C (41 °F) **and** its thermostat has been idle for 30 minutes | It tells you. |

The room in the message is looked up when the automation fires, so one playbook covers
every detector in the house.

A playbook with nothing left to do but notify is not offered, because brAIn already sends
those alerts. Freeze is the exception: it is a notification by design.

## Read the card before you apply

![The smoke playbook card with Details open: Every light to full brightness (12) with each light named, Heating and cooling off (2), Blinds and curtains open (1), Tells you, Skipped: protected — Bedroom curtains, and the line "This will not unlock any door and will not disarm the alarm — a false smoke alarm at 3am must not open the house." Rehearse it shows "12 → on (4 already on)" with each light's current state](./images/act-playbook-smoke.webp)

Open **Details**. Every entity is there by name, grouped by what happens to it.

| Line | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Runs when any of N sensors goes off** | The detectors that trigger it, and their rooms. |
| **Every light to full brightness (12)** | A group, its count, and the names. Past 12 names the card says how many more. The automation itself always includes all of them. |
| **Tells you** | Where the notification goes. |
| **Skipped: protected** | A device on your `protected_entities` list. brAIn knows it is there and will not touch it. |
| **No trial** | Why there is no trial week. Your last week had no emergency in it. |

### Check which way each valve closes

Some water switches close the supply when switched **on**. brAIn turns those on in a leak,
and lists them under their own line: **Water switches on (on closes these)**. Ordinary
shutoff switches are under **Water switches off**.

Read both lists before you apply. A switch brAIn has read wrong would be thrown the wrong
way in a leak.

brAIn finds shutoffs like this:

- Anything in the `valve` domain counts.
- A switch counts when brAIn has [read the device](/brain/checks/) as a water shutoff, in
  any language. A Dutch `hoofdkraan` qualifies.
- Where brAIn is not sure, it goes by whole words in the name or entity id: `water`,
  `valve`, `stopcock`, `mains` or `shutoff`. `main` alone does not count, so
  `switch.main_bedroom_lamp` is never mistaken for one. A shutoff named only "Main" is
  missed.

## Rehearse it

<Steps>

1. Open **Details** on the card.
2. Open **Rehearse it — show me what it would do**.
3. Read each call against the current state of its target: *12 → on (4 already on)*,
   *1 → closed: Mains water valve — open*.

</Steps>

A rehearsal reads the house and executes nothing. It answers one question: is this list
right?

To test the real thing, set the detector off on purpose and read the automation's trace
afterwards. Do not use Home Assistant's **Run actions** to test a playbook. That runs the
emergency.

## Apply, undo, ignore

**Apply** writes an ordinary automation to your `automations.yaml`, with an id that says
what it is, such as `brain_playbook_smoke`. It follows the same path as every suggestion:
written, reloaded, checked, put back if it did not take, with **Undo** on the toast and
`brain undo` later. See [Suggestions](/brain/proposals/#saying-yes-and-taking-it-back).

Playbooks have no **⋯ → Run**. Rehearse it instead.

**Ignore** is remembered by the change, not the wording. If you later fit another detector,
the automation is different and the playbook is offered again.

An applied playbook that has never fired is healthy. The "has never run"
[house check](/brain/checks/) does not flag it, or any other automation that watches a
smoke, gas, moisture or CO sensor.

## What decides the list

Your registries decide which detectors, lights, thermostats, covers and valves are in a
playbook. No model picks them.

Claude writes one optional thing: the plain-English paragraph on the card. It is a
scheduled run, so it waits while brAIn is paused, signed out or over its usage budget.
Until it runs, the card shows a sentence composed from the list.
