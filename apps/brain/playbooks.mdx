Everything else brAIn does either reports or proposes. This is the part that **acts**, and
it is deliberately last: the right to change something without asking has to be earned by
everything above it working first.

## Emergency playbooks

A playbook is a short, written response to one bad thing, built from what **your** house
actually has:

| Event | What a playbook does |
| --- | --- |
| **Smoke** | Unlock the doors it is allowed to, turn every light to full, stop the HVAC so it stops feeding air, announce on every speaker, tell everyone |
| **Leak** | Close the shut-off valve if there is one, name the sensor and the room, kill power to the machine if it is on a smart plug |
| **Power cut** | Report what is still up, what is on battery, what will not survive the night |
| **Freeze** | From [the thermal model](/brain/climate/) — which rooms reach the floor first, and what heat is available |

brAIn writes it from your devices, you read it once and accept it, and you can **rehearse**
it on demand — it walks the steps and reports what it *would* do, changing nothing.

:::caution[A playbook you have not rehearsed is a document, not a plan]
The rehearsal is the point. It is where you find that the "shut-off valve" is a switch that
was renamed in March.
:::

### The rules it acts under

Every action a playbook takes is:

- **Bounded.** It does the steps it was accepted with, and nothing it thought of at the time.
- **Reversible.** What it changed is recorded, and `brain undo` puts it back.
- **Announced.** You are told what it did, not asked to notice.
- **Refused for a protected entity.** Locks, alarms and anything in `protected_entities` are
  enforced in the MCP server — the one chokepoint every path to your house crosses — and not
  in a prompt that can be talked around.

## Overnight self-healing

A small set of remediations brAIn is allowed to attempt while you sleep, each with a
rollback and a line in the morning saying what it did:

- Reload an integration that has stopped answering.
- Restart an add-on that is `boot: auto` and stopped.
- Re-enable a helper that something disabled.
- Re-interview a Z-Wave node that has gone dead.

And a morning line: *"Overnight I reloaded the Hue integration, which had stopped answering
at 02:14. Everything else was quiet."*

The list is deliberately short and deliberately dull. Nothing here changes the *state of your
house* — no lights, no locks, no climate. Every entry is an infrastructure action whose worst
case is that it did not work, and each one is capped at a single attempt so a failing
integration does not get restarted forty times before breakfast.

:::note[It is off by default]
Self-healing ships **off**. Turning it on is a decision about how much you trust everything
above it, and that is not a decision an add-on should make on your behalf.
:::

## Scene designer

The everyday version of what BRight, the music light-show add-on, does for a party.

brAIn knows your rooms, what is in them, and what you actually set them to — the Activity
tab has been recording it. The scene designer proposes the scenes your house is missing:

> *You put the living room into the same four-light arrangement most evenings after 8pm.
> Here it is as a scene called **Evening**, with the trigger that usually precedes it.*

It reads the light map the same way the automation miner reads the logbook: **sets of states
you put the house into together, that recur**. Each proposal is a scene you can preview,
rename, edit, or decline — it goes through the [Proposals](/brain/proposals/) lifecycle like
everything else brAIn offers.

What it will not do is invent a look. A scene is a thing you have already demonstrated a
preference for; a scene brAIn imagined is a scene nobody uses.

## Reading the diff

This is the one release worth opening the pull request for rather than trusting green CI.
The tests are written the other way round from usual — **the refusals are tested harder than
the actions**, because a playbook that fails to fire costs you one incident report, and a
playbook that fires wrong costs you a house you no longer trust.

Every guard in it is verified against the failure it exists for: the test suite mutates the
guard, watches the test fail, and restores it. A guard that passes whether or not it is there
is not a guard.
