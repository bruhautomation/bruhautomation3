Most of brAIn is about your house. These jobs are about Home Assistant itself: the names
nobody tidied, the devices in no room, the updates you are not sure about, the error log nobody
reads, and the knowledge of how the house works that lives in one person's head.

| Job | Where it shows |
| --- | --- |
| [Names, rooms and aliases](#names-rooms-and-aliases) | A **Tidy-up** card on Insights → Needs you |
| [Updates waiting](#updates-waiting) | A card per assessed update on Needs you |
| [Overnight health check](#overnight-health-check) | Root causes on Needs you; **Run** in ⚙ → Diagnostics |
| [The house book](#the-house-book) | **Memory → House book** |
| [Who can reach the house](#who-can-reach-the-house) | A weekly sentence in ⚙ → Diagnostics, and access checks on Needs you |

<Aside type="note" title="Nothing in Home Assistant changes on its own">
A tidy-up is a list you tick; a verdict on an update is advice; the overnight check files cards;
the house book is a page. The only thing here that writes to Home Assistant is **Apply** (and
**Undo**) on a names-and-rooms card.
</Aside>

## Names, rooms and aliases

brAIn reads the house's own naming style first, a sample of the names *you* chose, then
suggests tidier names, rooms and spoken aliases as one **Tidy-up** card reading *Change ready*:

| Kind | What it fixes |
| --- | --- |
| **Rename** | Names that are still the hardware's (`0x00158d0001a2b3c4`, `Shelly Plus 1PM 8A4C`), which are unfindable in a picker and unsayable to Assist |
| **Room** | Entities and devices in no area, which "turn off the kitchen" and every area card cannot see |
| **Alias** | Spoken aliases for the things people actually say |

Every change is a ticked row under the card's **Details**. Untick what you disagree with, or
press **Change** on a row to say what it should be instead (a different name, or a room from your
own list); what you type is checked the same way brAIn's suggestion was. Then press **Apply**.

- **A room move says what it changes.** A row that moves a device names it, and if the move
  changes what an automation reaches, the card says so and Details names the automation.
- **Apply re-reads every field** from Home Assistant before writing and records only what Home
  Assistant accepted, so a batch that half-applied undoes exactly the half that happened.
- **Undo** is in **History › Done** for 30 days, and never overwrites a rename you made since.
- The *named after its hardware* and *no room* problems run this from **Plan**.

## Updates waiting

Each pending update brAIn has read the release notes for is a card on Needs you saying **safe
tonight**, **wait** (with the edit you would need to make first) or **not sure**, with the line
from the notes and the line from your configuration it relied on under **Details**. **Add to To
Do** puts the update on To Do.

**A *safe* or *wait* has to quote its evidence**: one line from the release notes and one line
from your configuration, and both are checked **word for word** against what was actually
read. An answer that cannot quote both is shown as *can't tell*, and so are notes that could
not be fetched — a verdict on notes nobody read is a guess, so no run is spent on one.

The release notes are treated as data, never as instructions, and the run that reads them
has no tools at all.

:::caution[What it never does]
- **It never installs anything.** There is no button here that runs an update.
- **It never reads `secrets.yaml`.** Any line in your configuration whose key looks like a
  credential is blanked before it is sent, and Home Assistant's own storage and the
  recorder's database are skipped.
:::

## Overnight health check

Once a night, between **03:00 and 06:00**, and whenever you press **Run** beside *Overnight health check* in ⚙ → Diagnostics,
brAIn reads:

- Home Assistant's error log;
- the Zigbee mesh — routers, weak links, devices not seen;
- Z-Wave node statistics and node status;
- automations that switch a smart plug feeding a Zigbee router.

It asks for **at most five root causes**, named as causes rather than symptoms:

> *The hallway plug that the upstairs Zigbee sensors route through keeps being switched off by
> the bedtime automation.*

**Every cause must cite the records it rests on**, by an id brAIn handed out — a log line, a
Zigbee device, a Z-Wave node, an automation. A cause that cites nothing, or cites a record that
was not there, is dropped and counted. The causes reach Needs you through the same
[first look](/brain/checks/) every finding takes.

A cause re-worded tomorrow is the same finding, not a new card every night: it is keyed by the
records it cites. And on a night a source could not be read, the findings that rest on it stay
put — *I could not look* is not *it went away*.

The scheduled run answers to the same switches every scheduled run does: a Claude sign-in,
**Automatic insights**, and the usage budget. A night with nothing in the log or the mesh worth
a look costs nothing.

## The house book

**Memory → House book** is how this house works, chapter by chapter: what runs on its own,
lights and scenes, heating and cooling, locks and security, what to do when an alarm goes off,
shutoffs and where things are, appliances, media and voice, and anything else worth knowing. It
is written from your own automations, scripts, scenes and what brAIn has learned, for a partner,
a sitter, or the person buying the house.

![Memory → House book, headed "How this house works", with an Add info button, the line "Every sentence names what it came from; codes and passwords are left out", and an empty-book message explaining that Add info with a blank box writes one from your automations, devices and what brAIn has learned](./images/panel-housebook.webp)

- **Add info** (top right, and on every chapter) opens a box. Type what you want documented
  (*the stopcock is under the kitchen sink*, *explain the bedtime routine*) and brAIn writes it in,
  cited to you. Leave it blank and brAIn fills the book, or that chapter, in from the house.
  **Add as written** puts your exact words in without a Claude run.
- **Every sentence names what it came from**, and **codes and passwords are left out**, always.
- **Every line has Edit and Delete.** A line you wrote or corrected is kept when brAIn rewrites
  the book; only the lines nobody touched are replaced.
- **Search, a room picker and chapter pills** narrow the book. Chapters with nothing in them yet
  are listed under *Not written yet*.
- **Gaps become questions** on Needs you, such as *where is the water shutoff?* Your answer goes
  into memory as your own words, so the next book knows.
- **It keeps itself current.** After the first book, brAIn rewrites it weekly, but only when
  something it reads has changed.

### Sharing it, and taking it back

**⋯ → Share a copy** gives you a private link to a read-only, redacted copy with no script,
under your Home Assistant's `/local` folder, and asks search engines to keep away. A shared copy
is kept current. **Stop sharing** deletes the page **and changes the link**, so a link somebody
kept stops working. The book's link has its own secret, separate from your shared insight cards.

## Who can reach the house

Four [house checks](/brain/checks/#rules-worth-knowing-about) look at access, and file
ordinary findings:

| Check | What it reports |
| --- | --- |
| `sec.lock_cloud_voice` | A lock or alarm panel exposed to Alexa or Google through Home Assistant Cloud |
| `sec.addon_unprotected` | An add-on whose protection mode has been switched off |
| `sec.login_bans` | Recent failed logins that got an address banned |
| `sec.brain_posture` | A terminal session still acting without asking after *Let brAIn act without asking* was turned **off** — turning it on is your choice and files nothing |

Each says nothing when it could not look, and **Ignore** with a reason stops it for that lock
or add-on.

Once a week brAIn also writes **one sentence** about who and what can reach the house, from
what the last checks pass read — no new look at the house of its own. It shows in ⚙ → Diagnostics under *Who can reach the house*, with when it was written;
**Run** writes it on demand. It answers to the same switches as every
scheduled run, and a review that failed waits six hours before trying again rather than
spending a run every few minutes.
