Morph is built to be changed by saying so. **Hold anything — a card, a room, a floor, the
whole dashboard — and tell brAIn what should be different.** brAIn looks at exactly what
you are looking at, makes the change, and shows you the card before and after, with
**Keep** and **Undo**. And for the times you would rather do it yourself, there is an
ordinary Edit mode.

## Tell brAIn

![Morph in Edit mode, dark theme: YAML and Done in brAIn's header, a hint line reading "hold anything to tell brAIn what to change · drag a section by its title · tap a card to rename, resize or remove it", the Ask line now reading "Tell … how to change this dashboard", and each room with a drag handle and its own Edit](./images/morph-edit-dark.webp)

<Steps>

1. **Press the pencil** (**Edit**) at the right of the page's first line.

2. **Hold** what you mean: a card, a section, a floor, or the whole dashboard. The Tell box
   opens.

3. **Look at "What brAIn will see".** The moment you hold something, Morph takes a picture
   of it and shows it in the box. brAIn reads that picture before anything else, so *"this
   is cut off"* or *"the colours are wrong"* mean what is on your screen.

4. **Say what should change**, and press **Send**.

</Steps>

You do not need Edit at all: type the change into the **Ask** line — *"remove the Coming up
timeline"*, *"the kitchen header is too tall"* — and brAIn reads it as a change to the
dashboard or to Morph itself and makes it as a Tell.

### You can watch it work

brAIn's own steps appear in the box as it takes them — looking at the picture, reading
`dashboard.yaml`, checking an entity, editing a file — with how long it has been at it.
Close the box and the work carries on: a strip at the top of the dashboard says what brAIn
is doing, and **Show** opens it again.

### It says where every change landed

When brAIn answers, Morph commits the dashboard half as **one change** and shows what brAIn
did, what it saw, and the card **before and after**. The result has one line for each place
a request could reach, whether or not anything happened there:

| Line | What it reports |
| --- | --- |
| **Dashboard** | What is different on screen — worked out by reading the dashboard back from the file, not from what brAIn said. |
| **Home Assistant** | Any change to the house itself (only when you asked for one). |
| **Morph's code** | Shown when Morph's own code changed. |
| **brAIn's memory** | Facts about the house it queued along the way. |
| **GitHub** | *Nothing sent* — unless you say yes to an issue. |

If nothing changed, it says *Nothing on the dashboard changed* and quotes brAIn. If brAIn
left `dashboard.yaml` unreadable, it says *Not applied* — Morph keeps showing the last
version that read — and **Undo** puts the file back. If your screen has not caught up,
**Refresh** loads it.

### Keep, Undo, Tell it more

- **Keep** — done.
- **Undo** — reverts exactly that commit, and if you asked for a change in Home Assistant,
  asks brAIn to put that back too.
- **Tell it more** — keeps the conversation above, takes a new picture of how it looks now,
  and asks what should be different. It is a conversation, not a one-shot.

### A choice that is yours comes back as buttons

The commonest one: a card that works a number out from other sensors — a difference, a
total, an efficiency — has no history, because Home Assistant only records entities. When
you might want that history, brAIn builds the card with the number worked out in it and
asks: **Calculate it in the card**, or **Make a Home Assistant sensor** so it is recorded.
The button that changes Home Assistant says so, and pressing one sends it as your next
request.

## What a Tell may change

**brAIn acts at once on Morph's own things:**

- the dashboard, `/config/morph/dashboard.yaml`;
- Morph's card code, `/config/morph/cards/`;
- Morph itself — its header, its layout, its built-in cards, its styles (see
  [Changing Morph itself](#changing-morph-itself)).

**It changes Home Assistant itself — entity ids, names, areas, automations, scripts,
helpers — only when you ask for that in words**: *"change the entity ID"*, *"rename the
device"*, *"make an automation that…"*. When a request would need a house change you did not
ask for, brAIn does the dashboard part and lists what would have to change under **Left for
you**, so you can ask.

<Aside type="note" title="brAIn's safety still holds">
Whatever you ask, brAIn's [protected entities](/brain/safety/), its action gate and its edit
snapshots (`brain undo`) still apply. What brAIn learns about the house along the way goes
into brAIn's own memory, not Morph's lessons.
</Aside>

## Edit mode

Press the quiet pencil (**Edit**) at the right of the page's first line, under brAIn's
header. **Done** and **YAML** are in the header while you edit.

| Do | To |
| --- | --- |
| **Tap a section** | Rename it, recolour it, move it to another floor (or a new one), widen it, add a group, hide it or remove it. |
| **Tap a card** | Rename it, resize it, move it, edit its JSON, or remove it. |
| **Drag a section** | Reorder it, or move it between floors. |
| **Add card** (in each section) | Offers the entities in that room first, then any entity, then **Lovelace card** — paste any card's YAML or JSON. |
| **YAML** (in the header) | Opens the dashboard as YAML. |
| **Hold anything** | Tell brAIn. |

**Every save is one commit.** The toast after a change offers **Undo**.

## History and undo

`/config/morph/` is a **git repository**. Every change — yours in Edit, brAIn's in a Tell,
the learning loop's, and an edit you make to the files by hand — is one commit, with who made
it.

- **⚙ › Dashboard › History** lists every change with who made it, and **Undo** or
  **Restore** for each. An undo is itself a commit, so an undo can be undone.
- **Edit the files by hand** if you like. Morph commits your edit as *Edited outside Morph*.
- **A broken file never blanks the page.** If `dashboard.yaml` stops parsing, the panel keeps
  showing the last version that did, with the parser's message and line above it, until the
  file is fixed.

## Changing Morph itself

Nothing about Morph is out of reach. Morph's own panel is a handful of modules that ship with
brAIn. To change one, brAIn **copies it into `/config/morph/frontend/` and edits the copy**,
and Morph serves your copy in place of its own after a page reload.

- **It is one change like any other.** It is in History, and Undo puts Morph's own back.
- **A copy that does not load is not a blank page.** Morph runs its own copy for that page
  load and says what went wrong.
- **An update can pause a copy.** If a brAIn update changes a file you changed, or a module
  your copy builds on, your change is **paused** and Morph's own runs — an old copy beside
  new modules is how a panel breaks. A note says so, with **Update** (merges your changes into
  the new version, as one change you can undo) and **Not now**. Where the update rewrote the
  same code your change was in, brAIn does not guess: it asks how to bring them together, with
  a button for each way.
- Your dashboard and your own cards (`cards/`) are **never touched by an update**.

When a Tell changes Morph's code, the box offers **Reload to see it** and **Undo**, then asks
**Would you like to open an issue to update Morph's code?** so everybody can have it.
**Nothing is sent unless you press Open an issue.**

### When Morph's own code fails

A card that fails to draw, a layout that overlaps at rest, or an answer from brAIn that Morph
could not read is a fault in Morph, not in your dashboard, and Morph records it. A request
that needs Morph's code changed is recorded only when you press **Open an issue** or **Send as
issue**. brAIn being slow or signed out is not Morph's fault and is not recorded.

Nothing leaves your house unless brAIn's **Development loop** switch (⚙ › Features) is on.
With it on, brAIn files each fault once in your private reports repository, redacted, where
the fix is made in Morph's code and arrives as an update.

## Use cases

**"This is cut off."** On the wall tablet, the Media card's title is truncated. You hold it,
type *this is cut off*, and brAIn — reading the picture of your screen, not a guess — widens
the card. You see before and after and press Keep.

**A total with history.** You ask for a card showing the whole house's power draw. brAIn
builds it, then asks: **Calculate it in the card**, or **Make a Home Assistant sensor**? You
want next month's chart, so you press the sensor button; brAIn says it will create a template
sensor, and does.

**"Rename the device" vs "rename the card".** *"Call this card Counter"* renames the card on
the dashboard. *"Rename the device to Kitchen counter"* renames it in Home Assistant — because
you said so in words.

**A header switch.** You want the porch light in Morph's header. No built-in card can do
that, so brAIn changes your copy of Morph's header module, offers **Reload to see it**, and
asks whether to open an issue so the feature can ship for everyone. You say no; it stays
yours.
