brAIn keeps one memory of your home: preferences, nicknames, household patterns, device
quirks. It is what turns *"turn on the beacon"* into the office lamp. The terminal, the
chat, voice, insight cards and house checks all read it, and you can see and change every
line.

## What ends up in it

Facts that change an answer:

- *"The office" means the upstairs back bedroom, not the study downstairs.*
- *Sam is a light sleeper. Nothing in the hallway should go above 15% brightness between
  23:00 and 06:00.*
- *Heating is an 8 kW air-source heat pump on weather compensation. "Boost the heating"
  means raising the flow temperature.*
- *The garage fridge is meant to run 24/7. Its draw at 3 am is the defrost cycle, not a
  fault.*
- *Never rename an entity_id without asking. Several are referenced in a Node-RED flow I
  cannot see.*

The last kind is a rule. Put the things you would otherwise repeat in every request here.

## Browse what brAIn knows

**Memory → Knowledge** lists every fact.

| To | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| See what it knows about one room or device | Pick it from the list on the left (a picker on a phone). |
| Find a fact | **Search facts**. A device's friendly name works. |
| Narrow the list | The **Rooms**, **Devices** and **House** pills, **Taught by** (anyone, you, a conversation, voice…) and the sort (newest, by room or device, oldest, most certain). |
| Remove a wrong fact | The bin beside it. |
| See where a fact came from | Each fact records who taught it (voice, the chat, the terminal, a run) and the conversation it came from. |
| Have brAIn review its own memory | **Clean up**. brAIn reads its memory and lists what looks wrong, useless or mistaken. |

Nothing on Knowledge waits on you, so it has no badge. What brAIn has *measured* (when the
house wakes, what is normal here) is in ⚙ → Diagnostics → Measurements. See
[Measurements](/brain/measurements/).

## Teach it

| Way | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Type it** | **Tell brAIn about the house…** and **Teach** on Memory → Knowledge: *"the garage freezer is off in winter"*. |
| **Say it** | *"Remember that…"* to a voice assistant. |
| **Talk normally** | After each turn in the chat or the terminal, a small run with no tools reads what *you* said and files anything durable: a preference, a nickname, a correction. It needs two new messages, or one that plainly teaches something. |
| **From the command line** | `brain memory add "…"` |
| **From an automation** | `brain.add_memory` |

brAIn also learns without being told:

- **From voice.** When a voice conversation ends, a short pass with no tools extracts up to
  three durable facts. Transient states, one-off commands and secrets are excluded. A
  preference stated by somebody signed in to Home Assistant (*"I like the bedroom at 19"*)
  is remembered as theirs.
- **By studying.** `brain learn <topic>`, the `brain.study` service or `/learn` in the
  terminal runs a deep session on one subject. With no topic it picks whatever has gone
  stalest.
- **From insight runs**, which file their durable findings the same way.

New facts are used straight away and written into the memory document once a day, or
sooner when 20 or more are waiting.

## Answer its guesses

brAIn states what it **believes** and asks you to confirm:

> *"The garage fridge is meant to run 24/7, right?"*

Guesses appear on [Needs you](/brain/needs-you/) under **Questions for you**.

| Press | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Yes** | It becomes a plain fact in memory. |
| **No** | A dead end that is never revisited, in any wording. Say why if you can: the reason is kept as a correction. |
| **Snooze** | It leaves every screen and stops counting against the limit, so brAIn can ask something else. Its fortnight starts again when it returns. |
| **Doesn't apply** | The card closes and nothing is recorded. |

Three rules hold in code, whatever a model does:

- At most **3 open** at a time.
- An unanswered guess expires after **14 days**.
- A guess is **never proposed again**, confirmed or rejected, however it is reworded.

When brAIn wonders why you did something by hand and three guesses are already open, it
waits for a free slot before spending anything. It never asks why about a lock, an alarm
panel or where people are.

Guesses from Home Assistant:

- `binary_sensor.brain_memory_questions_waiting` (*Questions waiting*) turns on when a guess
  needs an answer, with the open guesses and their ids in its attributes. Use it to push a
  guess to a phone before it expires.
- `brain.answer_question` answers one. Start the answer with *yes* or *no*.

## Rules you set

Pressing **Ignore** on a [house check's card](/brain/needs-you/#ignore-the-answer-that-teaches)
writes a rule (this sensor, this check) that the check reads on its next pass.

- A correction can reach further (the whole device, the room, everywhere) and can last
  until a date, a month or a season. Wide ones are asked first.
- They are listed under **Rules you set**. **Undo**, or *Let brAIn raise it again*, removes
  the rule.
- Only **Ignore** is permanent. *Done* and a finished to-do stop a card being raised only
  for as long as the problem stays fixed.

brAIn also writes short **judgements** about where its own calls keep disagreeing with you:
*"Night motion on the hall landing is the cat. 4 of 4 it raised were marked not a
problem."* The counts are arithmetic, not a model's guess. A judgement expires after 30
days unless renewed, goes away when a later card on the same thing turns out to be real,
and can be deleted like any fact.

<Aside type="caution" title="What a judgement never does">
It never makes brAIn quieter about a leak, smoke, gas, a freeze or anything protected, and
it is never about a person's health, sleep or whereabouts.
</Aside>

## Read, edit and undo the document

**Memory → Knowledge → Tools** holds the memory document.

| Under Tools | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **What brAIn has written down** | The document, with **Edit** for the raw text and **Export**. Below it, **Waiting to be filed** lists every queued fact and where it came from. ✕ drops one before it reaches memory. |
| **What brAIn is told before it works** | What each kind of run (a chat, a card, voice, a check) is handed, and what it can look up. |
| **Review the whole house** | The [deep review](/brain/insights/#beyond-cards-the-weekly-report-and-a-deep-review). |

The document is `/config/.brain/memory/memory.md`, plain markdown, and it is yours. Your
edits are the source of truth, and brAIn merges new facts around them.

Everything is also on the command line:

```bash
brain memory list                        # print the document
brain memory add "Guests use the loft"   # teach it something
brain memory forget "the loft is warm"   # queue a line for removal
brain memory edit                        # open memory.md in $EDITOR

brain memory log                         # what changed recently, and why
brain memory history                     # every kept version
brain memory undo                        # go back one version
brain memory undo 2                      # revert change #2 from the log

brain memory hypotheses                  # guesses waiting on a yes/no
brain memory confirm "<text>"            # yes: file it as a fact
brain memory reject "<text>" "why"       # no: a dead end, with your reason

brain memory inbox                       # facts waiting to be filed
brain memory consolidate                 # file them now
brain memory clear --confirm             # reset (old file kept as memory.md.bak)

brain learn energy                       # study a topic now
brain learn                              # study whatever is stalest
```

Forgetting reaches everything. A line you delete from the document, a
`brain memory forget`, a cleared memory, the ✕ on a queued fact and an **Undo** all reach
what every run is told, not only the file you can see. Your own corrections and the rules
an *Ignore* made survive a document that does not quote them.

<Aside type="note" title="Two different undos">
`brain memory undo` reverts a change to what brAIn *knows*. Plain
[`brain undo`](/brain/cli/#undo) reverts a change Claude made to a *file* in your
`/config`. They are separate journals.
</Aside>

## Move it to new hardware

```bash
brain memory export                      # on the old box: one JSON file
brain memory import backup.json          # on the new one
```

The export carries the memory document, the [Needs you](/brain/needs-you/) work list, the
answers you gave, the facts and the rules your *Ignore* presses made. **Export** under
Tools downloads the same file.

Import is command-line only, and it is a migration, not a sync:

- Your existing entries win where the two disagree.
- The memory document replaces only an effectively empty one, unless you pass
  `--replace-memory`.
- Importing the same file twice changes nothing the second time.

## Use it from Home Assistant

```yaml
# Teach a fact from an automation (administrators only when called by a signed-in user)
action: brain.add_memory
data:
  fact: "The dog gets fed at 7 and 17. Kitchen lights on then means feeding time"
  confidence: high     # high | medium | low (default medium)

# Study a topic in the background; results arrive in memory, not in a response
action: brain.study
data:
  topic: "energy"      # omit to study whatever has gone stalest
```

| Entity or event | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `brain_learned` | A logbook event for every new fact: *"brAIn learned: the hallway sensor drops offline around 2am"*. |
| `sensor.brain_memory_facts_learned` | How many things it knows. |
| `sensor.brain_memory_last_learned` | The most recent one, with the text as an attribute. |
| `binary_sensor.brain_memory_questions_waiting` | On while a guess needs an answer. |
| `sensor.brain_findings_open_findings` | How many cards are waiting on you, with the severity split and the texts. |
| `brain_finding` | Fires for every new finding, and can ring your phone. See [notifications](/brain/notifications/). |

## Turn learning off

| Switch | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **⚙ → Features → Learning loop** | The master switch. Off stops the voice pass, what the terminal and chat teach, and the rest of [learning](/brain/learning/). Existing memory stays and is still used. |
| `memory_injection: false` | Keep learning, but stop adding memory to voice prompts. |
| `brain memory clear --confirm` | Forget everything learned so far. |

## How memory is stored

| What | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `memory.md` | The document. Plain markdown, editable, capped at `memory_max_kb` (default 32 KB, range 1–64). |
| `voice.md` | A summary of at most 2 KB that voice reads on every request. Regenerated, so do not edit it. |
| The inbox | A queue of facts waiting to be filed. |
| Guesses | A queue of open guesses. |
| The change log | An audit trail, so you can see what changed and undo it. |
| Facts | The same knowledge one fact at a time, tagged with what it is about, who taught it and when. This is what Knowledge shows and what a run looks things up in. |

All of it lives under `/config/.brain/memory/`. The queues and the change log are never
put into a prompt.

- **One writer.** Only the daily filing pass writes `memory.md`. It dedupes, lets the
  newest fact win a contradiction and holds the size cap.
- **It cannot quietly erase what it knows.** A pass that would drop more than 60% of the
  document is refused, and a failed pass leaves memory untouched with the queue still
  waiting. Both files are replaced whole or not at all.
- **Runs get the facts that matter.** A run is handed facts about the rooms and devices it
  is reading, with facts that share words with your question first.
- **Duplicates merge.** The same fact said two ways is stored once. Facts that differ by a
  "not" stay apart.
- **Some facts expire.** A note about a device's health (*"the hall sensor's battery is at
  12%"*) expires after thirty days, and a reading of one moment after two. Facts about an
  entity your house no longer has stop being used at once, are removed thirty days later,
  and come back if the entity does.
- **The cap is not a speed setting.** Voice reads the 2 KB summary, never the document. The
  cap decides how much brAIn can know before filing something new means dropping something
  old.
