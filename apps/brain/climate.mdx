brAIn measures how each room in your house holds heat, then tells you when the heating
starts too late, a window is open, a room never gets warm, or the pipes are at risk. You
set no thresholds.

![A Needs you card: "Bedroom is losing heat faster than it can". Bedroom temperature fell from 19.4 °C to 17.9 °C over the last 35 minutes, about 2.6 °C an hour, where this room's own measured insulation allows about 0.9 °C an hour at 3 °C outside. Fix: shut whatever is open](./images/think-card-window.webp)

## What it tells you

| What happened | The card | Urgency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A window or outside door is open in a heated room | *"Bedroom is losing heat faster than it can"* | Straight away, through quiet hours |
| A room is falling towards the temperature pipes are at risk at | *"Utility room is on course to get cold enough to matter"* | Straight away, through quiet hours |
| Tonight's forecast is near freezing | *"Near freezing the night of …: protect what holds water outside"* | The same day |
| The room is still warming up when you get up | *"Nursery is still warming up when this house gets up"*, with the time the heating would need to start | Held through quiet hours |
| A room is set to a temperature it has not reached in a month | *"Landing never reaches the temperature it is set to"* | Held through quiet hours |
| One room loses heat much faster than the others | *"Loft room loses heat much faster than the rest of the house"* | Held through quiet hours |

Checks that would name the same room stand down for each other, so one room gets one card.
`climate.underheated` wins over `climate.heat_loss`, and `climate.freeze` over
`climate.window`.

## What you need

| You need | Why |
| --- | --- |
| One outdoor temperature sensor | Every room is measured against it. brAIn [picks it](#the-outdoor-thermometer) and you can change it |
| At least one indoor thermometer in an area | So a card can name a room |
| Both in the same unit | A Fahrenheit reference against a Celsius room gives a wrong loss rate |
| About a month of history with some change in the weather | The model is fitted to 28 days of nights |

With no usable outdoor sensor there is no model, and **⚙ → Diagnostics** says so.

## Set up and check the model

1. Put your thermometers in areas in Home Assistant. Put the outdoor one in an area named
   as outdoors (a garden, a patio, a porch), or leave it in no area.
2. Wait for the nightly pass. Open **⚙ → Diagnostics → Measurements → How rooms hold heat**
   to see which rooms were measured and which sensor is the outdoor reference.
3. If the reference is wrong, pick a different sensor there. Every room is measured again
   straight away.

## The six checks

### The heating starts too late

`climate.preheat` reports a room that reaches its setpoint after the house is up, and
names the time the heating would need to start.

![A Needs you card: "Nursery is still warming up when this house gets up". On weekday mornings Nursery temperature reads about 17.2 °C at 06:50, when somebody is first up, and it is asked for 20 °C. Fix: move the heating schedule for this room earlier by about 1 hour 35 minutes](./images/think-card-preheat.webp)

Three measurements have to agree:

1. When this house gets up, from [rhythm](/brain/measurements/#rhythm-when-this-house-gets-up).
2. What the room reads at that hour of an ordinary week, from its baseline.
3. How long the climb takes, from the heat model.

It reports weekday mornings only, and only once your wake time has been measured. A room
still short two hours later is reported as underheated.

### Something is open

`climate.window` reports a room falling more than twice as fast as its own measured heat
loss allows at the current outdoor temperature.

### The pipes are at risk

`climate.freeze` takes the room's reading and the outdoor temperature and works out when
the room reaches **5 °C**. It reports a room that is already falling and will get there
within 12 hours. An **Ignore** does not stop this check reporting a heated room again.

### A cold night is forecast

`climate.frost_ahead` files one card when the forecast low for the next 24 hours is within
2 °C of freezing. It names what holds water where the heating does not reach: irrigation
and outdoor taps, a pool, and unheated spaces such as a garage or shed. It clears when the
night has passed.

### The heating never gets there

`climate.underheated` reports a room that has never once reached its setpoint in a month
of readings. It says nothing if the month held no cold night.

### One room loses heat faster

`climate.heat_loss` compares heated rooms with each other and reports one that empties
much faster than the rest. It needs at least four measured rooms. A garage or loft with
no heating is not compared.

## The outdoor thermometer

Every room is measured against one outdoor sensor. brAIn chooses it on evidence.

**Never used:**

- a thermometer in an area you have not named as outdoors;
- a dew point, feels-like, wet bulb or heat index sensor.

**Preferred, among the rest:**

- a sensor in an outdoor area, or in no area;
- a sensor from the same integration as your weather entity;
- a sensor that reads close to what your weather entity reads;
- after a month, the one whose readings swing through the day the way outside air does.

A sensor named like a heat pump's coil, a pool or a pipe counts against itself. Names are
read [in any language](/brain/checks/#reading-what-a-device-is-in-any-language).

To overrule the choice, open **⚙ → Diagnostics → Measurements → How rooms hold heat**. It
says which sensor won and why, lists the others, and lets you pick one. If your choice is
not a thermometer, brAIn says so and ignores it.

## Tell brAIn about a room or a sensor

- **A thermometer that is not in the room's air** (inside a pump box, on a manifold): press
  **Ignore** on its card and choose ***…doesn't measure the room's air: leave it out of room
  and heat checks***.
- **A room nothing heats**: tell brAIn the room is unheated, in a card's reason or under
  **Memory → Knowledge**. The window, freeze and heat-loss checks then leave it out, and
  the frost warning still covers it.

## How the model works

Two numbers are measured per room from 28 days of hourly history:

| Number | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **`k`**, loss per hour | How fast the room falls towards the outdoor temperature. Its reciprocal is the time constant: "this room holds its heat for about eight hours" |
| **`h`**, gain in degrees per hour | How fast the heating puts heat back |

- **`k` is measured between 01:00 and 05:00 only.** There is no sun then and, in most
  houses, no heating schedule.
- **A room is a reading that stays in a band people live in.** A freezer, a hot water tank
  or a barbecue probe has a temperature device class and is not measured as a room.
- **The live checks read five-minute history.** `climate.window` and `climate.freeze` need
  to see a window opened forty minutes ago, which an hourly average cannot show.
