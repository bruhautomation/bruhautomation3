Say what you want your house to do, in a sentence. brAIn writes the automation, checks it
against your own history, and puts it on **Insights → Needs you** for you to **Apply**.

![A Suggestion card titled "Whenever the front door opens after dark, turn the hall light on". It reads "You asked for this: … brAIn read that as: When the front door opens between sunset and sunrise, turn on the Hall light." Under Details, Replay: "Over the last 30 days it would have fired 9 times: 5 in the last 14 days, you had already done the same on 4, you did the opposite on 1." Presses: Apply, Snooze, Ignore](./images/act-rule.webp)

| You say | brAIn makes | It runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *"Whenever the front door opens after dark, turn the hall light on."* | A **rule** | Every time |
| *"Always turn the heating down when nobody is home."* | A **rule** | Every time |
| *"Turn the porch light off when the guests leave."* | A **one-off** | Once, then switches itself off |
| *"Tell me when the tumble dryer finishes."* | A **one-off** | Once, then switches itself off |

A sentence about now and later, such as *"water the garden for 90 minutes"*, is neither.
brAIn runs it itself as a timed action and writes no automation. See
[`timed_action`](/brain/mcp/).

## Where to say it

| Where | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Morph** | The *Ask or tell the house* line on the [Morph dashboard](/brain/morph/). |
| **Your phone** | **Reply** on a brAIn notification. |
| **Voice** | Say it to a [voice assistant](/brain/voice/). |
| **An automation or script** | The `brain.intent` service. |

```yaml
action: brain.intent
data:
  sentence: Turn the porch light off when the front door has been shut for ten minutes
```

None of these waits for the answer. brAIn has to search your house first, so the card
appears on Needs you shortly after. A sentence is cut at 300 characters.

In the [Ask](/brain/terminal/) chat, brAIn edits automations directly and says what changed.
For a new standing rule it can replay it over your history first, the same way.

When brAIn cannot read the sentence itself, the opening words decide. *When…*, *whenever…*,
*once…*, *as soon as…*, *the next time…*, *tell me when…*, *let me know when…*, *remind me
when…*, *every time…*, *always…* and *from now on…* mark an instruction. *"When did the
boiler last run?"* is still a question.

## Ask for a rule

<Steps>

1. Say the rule: *"Whenever the front door opens after dark, turn the hall light on."*
2. brAIn drafts the automation. It can search your house to find what you named. It cannot
   change anything.
3. Before you see it, brAIn simulates it twice. A replay of the last 30 days counts how
   often it would have fired. The last 14 days of those firings are graded against what you
   did by hand.
4. The card arrives with your sentence, what brAIn understood, and the result in one line:
   *Over the last 30 days it would have fired 9 times: 5 in the last 14 days, you had
   already done the same on 4, you did the opposite on 1.*
5. Press **Apply**, or **⋯ → Run** to [try it for a week](/brain/proposals/#try-it-for-a-week-first)
   first.

</Steps>

Your sentence and brAIn's reading sit side by side, so when a rule is wrong you can see
which half was misread. **Ignore** it and ask again in different words.

**Apply** writes the rule to `automations.yaml`, reloads, and checks Home Assistant created
it. The toast offers **Undo**, and `brain undo` works later. The automation's description
quotes your sentence (*Asked for in brAIn: "…" — accepted on …*), so the file still says
why it is there. The full path is on
[Suggestions](/brain/proposals/#saying-yes-and-taking-it-back).

Two details of how rules are written:

- A rule like *"turn the light off ten minutes after the last motion"* restarts its ten
  minutes on a second motion. A rule that would run several copies of itself at once is
  refused.
- Rules that use Home Assistant's newer editor triggers, such as *When a door opens*,
  [replay](/brain/replay/) like any other.

<Aside type="caution" title="When a rule is applied without a card">
With *Act* or *No limits* set in ⚙ → Permissions, a rule you typed is applied at once.
It goes through the same checks and gets the same Undo. If it cannot be applied, it stays
a card with the reason on it. See [Permissions & safety](/brain/safety/).
</Aside>

## Ask for a one-off

<Steps>

1. Say it: *"Turn the porch light off when the guests leave."*
2. A card arrives with your sentence, what brAIn understood, and a replay of how often that
   trigger would have fired over the last 30 days. Here the replay is a check that brAIn
   read the right trigger. It is not evidence that you want it.
3. Press **Apply**. Nothing runs before that.
4. The card moves to **Set to run once** at the bottom of Needs you, marked **Armed**.

</Steps>

![The Set to run once group on Needs you with three cards: "Turn the porch light off when the guests leave" marked Armed, with You asked and brAIn understood under Details and a Delete button; "Tell me when the tumble dryer finishes" marked It fired, reading "It fired at 9:02 PM and switched itself off. It is still in your automations until you remove it."; and a card marked Not armed with the reason and a Done button](./images/act-oneoffs.webp)

What gets written is an ordinary automation in your `automations.yaml`. Its last action
switches the automation itself off, so Home Assistant does the running and nothing in brAIn
has to stay alive for it. Its name is your sentence.

Cards under **Set to run once** are waiting on the house, not on you, so the badge never
counts them.

| Pill | What the card says | Press |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Armed** | *Armed and waiting. It runs once, then switches itself off.* | **Delete** |
| **Armed**, **Waiting a fortnight** | *It has been waiting a fortnight and has never fired. Remove it if what you were waiting for already happened.* | **Delete** |
| **It fired** | When it fired, and that it is still in your automations until you remove it. | **Delete** |
| **Not armed** | A sentence brAIn will not arm, and why. | **Done** |

brAIn learns that a one-off fired from the automation's own `last_triggered`, on the next
[house-checks pass](/brain/checks/). Switching it off by hand does not count as firing.

## Remove a one-off

Nothing removes a one-off for you. Not when it fires, and not after a fortnight.

<Steps>

1. Press **Delete** on the card and confirm.
2. brAIn cuts that one entry out of `automations.yaml`, reloads, and checks the automation
   and its registry entry are gone.
3. The toast says *Removed it from your automations* and offers **Undo**, which puts the
   automation and the card back.

</Steps>

If any step fails, the file is put back and the card says which step. The rest of your file
is not reformatted.

## What brAIn will not write

A refusal is a card, never a silent drop. For a one-off it sits under **Set to run once**
marked **Not armed**, and **Done** clears it.

| Refusal | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **A one-off that sounds like a standing rule**, or the reverse | The card shows what brAIn understood, so you can ask again the right way round. |
| **A trigger that cannot be replayed** | A webhook, a device trigger, or one aimed at a whole area. Without a replay there is nothing to check a new trigger against. `time`, `state`, `numeric_state`, `template` and the newer editor's triggers can be [replayed](/brain/replay/). |
| **The sentence named nothing brAIn could act on** | The automation would do nothing. |
| **It touches a protected entity** | Anything in `protected_entities`. See [Permissions & safety](/brain/safety/). |
| **Six one-offs are already armed** | Delete one and ask again. |
| **No automation could be worked out**, or what you named was not found | The card says what brAIn looked for. |

Only the five most recent refusals are kept. They never push armed one-offs off the list.

## Where to see it

| Where | What |
| --- | --- |
| **Insights → Needs you** | A drafted rule or one-off under **Chores and suggestions**. Armed, fired and refused one-offs under **Set to run once**. |
| **⚙ → Diagnostics** | How many one-offs are armed, fired, refused, overdue and still queued, with the cap and the fortnight. |
