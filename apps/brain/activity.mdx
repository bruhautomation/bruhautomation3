**Memory → Timeline** tells you what happened in your house and what caused it. It is
where *"why did the hallway light come on at 3am?"* gets an answer: an automation, a
person, a voice command, or brAIn.

## Read the Timeline

- **The top line** says what the house is doing now: who is home, which rooms are in use,
  which machines are running.
- **Events are grouped by kind of thing**: Home Assistant, heating and cooling, people,
  doors, media, motion, lights and switches. Each row has the device, what changed, when,
  how long, and the cause.
- **When the house was empty** is one line a day at the head of the list.
- **Noise is folded.** A Home Assistant restart and the devices that dropped out around it
  are one row. A device that kept flipping is one row with a count.
- **The foot** says how many sensor readings were left out (a reading is not something that
  happened) and how many changes have no recorded cause.

The Timeline is a record, not a queue, so it has no badge. It is fetched fresh every time
you open it.

## Find something

| To | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Find a device | **Search devices**. |
| Narrow by room, cause or kind of thing | **Filter**. Each choice counts what is under the others, and what is in force shows as tags with **Clear all**. |
| Change the window | **6 hours**, **12 hours**, **24 hours** or **2 days**. |
| Go back in time | **‹ Earlier** and **Later ›**. |
| Have brAIn read it for you | **Ask about the last 24 hours**. One Claude run reads what is on screen and says what it adds up to. Everything else on this tab costs nothing. |

## What each cause means

| Cause | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Person** | A named user did it, from the app or the UI. A change you asked for in brAIn's chat or terminal is yours too, marked *via brAIn*. |
| **Automation** | A rule fired. |
| **Script** / **Scene** | Something ran. |
| **Voice** | An assistant command: Home Assistant's Assist, or a brAIn voice agent. |
| **brAIn** | brAIn changed it unattended: a fix you approved, a background run. |
| *Nothing shown* | Unattributed. Nothing in the record says. |

Both the immediate and the root cause are kept. An automation somebody started from the UI
shows the automation *and* the user.

<Aside type="note" title="Unattributed is an honest answer">
A wall switch and a device's own integration reach Home Assistant the same way. Naming
either would be a guess, so brAIn shows no cause instead of the likeliest one.
</Aside>

## Spot an automation you keep undoing

An **override** is a person putting something back within a few minutes of an automation
moving it. The automation ran and nothing errored, so no ordinary check can see it. It is
the clearest sign that an automation is wrong for your house.

The row says so, as in *a person undid Evening lights*, and overrides ride above the list.

| Counts as an override | Does not |
| --- | --- |
| Turning off a light an automation just turned on. | Pressing "on" after a rule turned it on. That is agreement. |
| Putting it back by voice, through Assist or a brAIn agent. | An automation undoing an automation. That is a conflict, a different finding. |
| Nudging a dimmer three times after one automation move. It counts once. | |

Past three overrides of one automation in a day, brAIn files a card on
[Needs you](/brain/needs-you/) (the `auto.overridden` check). The card says how often the rule ran, because three undos
of a rule that ran three times means something different from three in three hundred.

## Ask about it

Ask *"what turned the porch light on last night?"* in [Ask](/brain/terminal/) or in Morph's
Ask line. Claude reads the same record through two read-only tools, `explain_change` and
`get_activity`, and answers with the chain of causes.

A voice agent at the default *Voice assistant* level cannot read house-wide activity. It
only reaches what you expose to Assist.

## How brAIn knows what it did

Home Assistant records what set off each state change, and the Timeline follows that
chain. brAIn's own calls look like any other integration's there, so brAIn keeps its own
ledger of every service call it makes and joins the two.

- Each ledger row says which face asked (voice, the chat, the terminal, or an unattended
  run) and the conversation it came from. That is how *"turn the hall light back on"*, said
  to brAIn, counts as you overriding the motion rule.
- A call aimed at a whole area or device is recorded as that, not expanded into entities.
  A wrong expansion would blame brAIn for somebody else's change.
- The ledger records each entity's state just before brAIn's call. A fix's
  [Restore](/brain/needs-you/#plan-apply-and-undo) uses that to set things back.
