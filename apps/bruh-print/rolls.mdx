## What is loaded

Tell BRUH Print which stock is in each roll and it refuses a label that does not match. Without
that check, a 2.25″-wide label sent to a 0.56″ roll prints across the backing paper — once per
copy, with no error anywhere.

**Nothing asks you which roll.** You pick the label; BRUH Print knows which bay it is in. There is
no roll picker on the Quick tab, in the designer, on a template, on the card or in the print
services — naming the stock has already named the bay.

**Only what is loaded can be picked.** Every label picker outside the Printer tab lists only the
rolls actually in the printer. The whole catalog lives on the Printer tab, where the question is
"what did I just load".

### Twin Turbo

On a **LabelWriter 450 Twin Turbo** you get two bays, **Left roll** and **Right roll**. The roll
chip at the top of every tab names both in words (*Left: Address Labels · prints text*), and the
roll that plain text prints on is marked **prints text** everywhere — on the panel, on the card and
in the printer sensor's `text_prints_on` attribute. Change it from the Quick tab's **Prints on**
line, from the card, or with `bruh_print.choose_label`; all three are the same setting. A printer
with one roll never shows a control for a second.

### How many are left

Each loaded roll says *"About N left"* or *"Not counting yet — tell me how many are on it"*, and
that line **is** the button that sets it. Nothing on a LabelWriter reports a real level, so the
count is an estimate counted down from prints — it drifts the moment somebody prints from another
machine, which is why it is a control and not just a readout. **Full roll** resets it. A low roll is
marked in the header, on the Printer tab and on the card. Turn **Keep an estimate of how many labels
are left** off under Settings and the count stops moving as well as showing.

## Lining up a roll

A LabelWriter finds the top of each label with a sensor looking for the punched hole in the gap
between labels, and where that hole sits relative to the label is punched into the **roll**. So a
given printer and roll can start laying ink a few millimetres after the label's leading edge, every
time — and no command moves it earlier. BRUH Print's answer is to find out where the printable area
really is, and lay labels out inside it.

<Steps>

1. Press **Line up this roll** on the bay that holds it, then **Print**. One label comes out with a
   numbered grid drawn across the whole print head and on past the end of the label — some ink
   lands on the backing and the next label. That overrun is the instrument.
2. Read where the label's four edges fall on the grid and type them in. The white numbers on the
   black strips run **across** the label (X); the black numbers in the plain columns run **down**
   it (Y). The end with the small numbers is the top, and came out first. Every scale has a number
   every 5 mm and a tick every 1 mm.
3. Press **Apply**. BRUH Print says, in one sentence, what your printer does with this roll.
4. Press **Print a check label**. It draws a frame around everything the roll can print on, with
   *"Should reach every edge"* inside. A complete frame means you are done; a missing side says
   which way it is out.

</Steps>

| Reading | What to read |
| --- | --- |
| **X1** | Where the label's **left** edge falls on a black strip |
| **X2** | Where the **right** edge falls — leave empty if the strip stops short (the label is wider than the print head) |
| **Y1** | Where the **top** edge falls in a plain column — **0 if the top edge is above the grid**, the common case |
| **Y2** | Where the **bottom** edge falls (the grid always runs past it) |

It is a loop, not a form: both prints sit at the top of the step you type into, and reopening a
roll that is already lined up brings its numbers back filled in, so a small correction is one box.
**Forget** throws the measurement away — back to nothing, not to a guess, so an un-lined-up roll gets
exactly the job BRUH Print has always sent.

**Where to print on it** is the other half of that step: a number per edge — **Top**, **Bottom**,
**Left**, **Right** — holding that much of the label blank on purpose. **Even up the ends** fills the
bottom one in for you, so the top and bottom borders match on a printer that starts a few
millimetres in. An even border on all four sides is the stock's own **margin** instead.

## Label stock

![The Label stock section: a note on which measurement is which, and stock rows such as Chemical-Resistant Cryo Labels, Cryogenic Labels, Address, Multipurpose, Return address, Shipping, Library spine and Jewellery, each with its size, border, part number, a Text direction picker, Rename or resize and Hide](./images/label-stock.webp)

A stock is two measurements, and they are **not** interchangeable:

- **Across the print head** — the width the head covers in one pass.
- **Along the roll** — how far the paper travels for one label.

Getting them the wrong way round is the most common way a label comes out rotated with the text off
the edge, and nothing can work it out for you. **Rename or resize → These are the wrong way round**
swaps them in one press, and lining up a roll offers the swap when its measurements look reversed.

**Rename or resize** also holds the roll's **name** (call it what you call it — every picker
follows), the blank border kept clear of the edge (2 mm by default), and how many labels are on a
full roll. Editing a built-in stock saves an override, so a future release cannot take your
measurement back.

**Text direction** belongs to the stock, not the job, and is asked once, here. A label much longer
than it is wide is a wrap-around label, so its text runs *along the roll* automatically, and the
picker says which way "automatic" decided.

The catalog ships with the common DYMO part numbers — 30252 address, 30256 shipping, 30323 large
shipping, 30336 multipurpose, 30330 return address, 30346 library spine, 30299 jewellery — two
cryogenic stocks, and continuous 2.25″ tape whose length comes from the artwork. **Add a stock** takes
a name and the two measurements; the part number, labels per roll and margin are optional.

## Settings

![The Settings card: If nothing comes out set to Standard — recommended, Darkness set to Dark — recommended, Print speed set to Slow & dark, and checkboxes for Refuse a label whose stock is not in the roll, Quick labels are UPPERCASE, and Keep an estimate of how many labels are left](./images/settings.webp)

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **If nothing comes out** | Standard | *Standard* is what everything is tested against. *Compact* adds a compression opcode not every firmware reads. *Bare minimum* also drops roll select (a Twin Turbo loses its second bay), darkness and speed — the last thing to try. |
| **Darkness** | Dark | How much heat the head puts into each dot. Turn it down if labels smudge or curl. |
| **Print speed** | Slow & dark | The printer's "barcodes and graphics" mode (300 × 600): darker and more accurate for barcodes and QR codes. *Fast* is the printer's own default and roughly halves the time of a long run. |
| **Refuse a label whose stock is not in the roll** | On | The stock check. Seeded from the `enforce_stock` option on first run. |
| **Quick labels are UPPERCASE** | Off | |
| **Keep an estimate of how many labels are left** | On | Off stops the count entirely. |

<Aside title="Why the margin is 2 mm">
The print head does not start at the edge of the backing, thermal stock curls at the die cut, and a
LabelWriter's registration wanders a fraction of a millimetre as the roll unwinds. At 1 mm, text
reaches the cut every other label.
</Aside>
