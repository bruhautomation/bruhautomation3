A WiFi-connected candle that ignites and extinguishes itself on command via Home Assistant. Arc lighter for ignition, diaphragm pump for blowout.

:::danger
This involves high voltage and open flame. Proceed at your own risk.
:::

<AffiliateDisclosure />

## Choosing a Candle

Standard wax candles are a poor fit for automation because the wick position changes as the candle burns down. You can't just aim a lighter at a fixed point and call it done.

For this prototype, I used an oil-filled candle with a fixed wick — the ignition point stays consistent relative to the base, which made everything much simpler.

## Ignition

I tested three different methods before finding one that worked reliably:

- **Resistance wire** — [32AWG Kanthal A1](https://amzn.to/3LvWkn8) heated up but was inconsistent and burned out quickly.
- **High voltage generator** — A [5V buck boost](https://amzn.to/3sZZnh4) was extremely loud and immediately destroyed the wick. Turns out stun guns and arc lighters work very differently — arc lighters use higher frequency, lower power pulses that transfer heat more efficiently.
- **Arc lighter** — A USB rechargeable [arc lighter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09YPZK854?tag=brau01-20) with opto-isolated relay control. This is what worked.

:::note
High-voltage modules need to be electrically isolated from your control circuitry. Otherwise you'll get random resets on the microcontroller.
:::

## Extinguishing

I went with a [5V diaphragm pump](https://www.amazon.com/NW-5V-6VDC-Miniature-Vacuum-100KPa/dp/B078H8V563?tag=brau01-20) — it has both an inlet and an outlet, so I positioned copper tubes on either side of the flame to simultaneously blow and suck the fire out.

The copper tubes double as the arc lighter conductors, so there's no additional hardware needed for ignition.

## Build

The controller is an ESP32 running ESPHome. Here's the parts list:

### Parts

- ESP32 ([one I used](https://amzn.to/3t2FTZm)) — these days I'd use a [screw-terminal breakout](https://amzn.to/45UUzIv)
- [Battery module](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B06XCXPY86?tag=brau01-20) + [18650 battery](https://amzn.to/3LvB2Gt) with [clip](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B00LSG5BKO?tag=brau01-20)
- NO [16mm toggle button](https://www.amazon.com/ARTGEAR/dp/B07KX144DT?tag=brau01-20) (power) + [momentary buttons](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B07KX144DT?tag=brau01-20) (ignite/extinguish)
- [Relay board](https://amzn.to/48nCtR6) or [single relays](https://amzn.to/3PwALnY)
- [Arc lighter](https://amzn.to/3sY6EOD)
- [Diaphragm pump](https://amzn.to/3PIcYTg)
- [Copper tube](https://amzn.to/3RsNtXs) + [silicone tubing](https://amzn.to/44YBIeb) + 3D-printed base

### Code

See the [ESPHome YAML on GitHub](https://github.com/bruhautomation/bruhautomation3/blob/main/projects/smart-candle/esphome/bruh-candle.yaml).

### Safety

I added a physical power switch between the battery and ESP32. The candle has to be manually armed before it can be remotely ignited — it can't accidentally turn on from a stray automation or API call.
