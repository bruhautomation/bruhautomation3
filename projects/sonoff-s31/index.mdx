The S31 is the smart plug worth buying, because it has a **CSE7766** metering
chip inside — real voltage, current, wattage and energy totals, not an estimate.
Flashed with ESPHome it reports all of that to Home Assistant locally, keeps
working when the internet is down, and never talks to a vendor server again.

It is also the easiest Sonoff to flash. The board slides out of the case and the
programming pads are a labelled, properly spaced header — no scraping at test
points with a probe.

## What you need

<AffiliateDisclosure />

- [Sonoff S31 smart plug](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Sonoff+S31+smart+plug&tag=brau01-20) — make sure it is the **S31**, not the S31 Lite, which has no metering chip
- [USB to serial adapter (3.3 V)](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=CP2102+USB+to+TTL+serial+adapter+3.3V&tag=brau01-20)
- [Pogo pins or jumper wires](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=pogo+pin+programming+jig+header&tag=brau01-20)
- A small Phillips screwdriver and a plastic prying tool

:::danger
The board is mains-powered in normal use. **Unplug it from the wall before you
open it, and never plug it into mains while the serial adapter is attached.**
Flash it on the bench, close it up, then plug it in.
:::

## Flashing

<Steps>

1. **Open it.** Remove the two screws under the label on the end, then slide the
   board out of the plastic shell.

2. **Find the four pads** — `3V3`, `RX`, `TX`, `GND` — in a row near the edge of
   the board.

3. **Wire the adapter**, crossing the data lines: adapter TX to board **RX**,
   adapter RX to board **TX**, then 3V3 to 3V3 and GND to GND.

4. **Hold the button down while connecting the adapter to USB.** That is what
   puts the ESP8266 into flash mode. Keep holding for a second or two after.

5. **Flash the config** below from ESPHome. After this first wired flash,
   everything is over WiFi.

6. **Close it up before it goes anywhere near a socket.**

</Steps>

## What the config gives you

The board is an `esp01_1m` — 1 MB of flash, which is why the config keeps the
logger quiet and the feature list tight.

| Function | GPIO | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Relay | **GPIO12** | The switch entity in Home Assistant |
| Status LED | **GPIO13** | Inverted, driven as a PWM light |
| Button | **GPIO0** | Inverted; also the flash-mode button |
| CSE7766 metering | **UART RX** | Serial, not GPIO — the chip streams readings |

In Home Assistant you get a switch, the LED as a dimmable light, and sensors for
voltage, current, power and cumulative energy. The energy sensor is a
`total_daily_energy`, so it plugs straight into the Energy dashboard.

## Troubleshooting

**The flash never starts.** The button has to be held *as* USB power arrives,
not after. Try again holding it from before you plug in until a second after.

**It flashes but the power readings are zero.** That is the S31 Lite — no
CSE7766. The relay and button will work; the metering never will.

**It flashes, then won't connect to WiFi.** The S31's antenna is inside a plastic
brick behind a mains transformer. Check signal strength before blaming the flash.

**Readings are slightly off.** The CSE7766 can be calibrated in the config —
worth doing if you're billing anything off it, unnecessary if you just want to
know the dryer finished.
