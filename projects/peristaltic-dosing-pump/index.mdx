Lab peristaltic pumps have a DB25 remote-control port on the back that almost
nobody uses, because the vendor's foot pedal and dosing controller cost more than
the pump did. This is that controller, built out of an Arduino and a laser-cut
box: dial in a volume, hit a footswitch, get the same dose every time — and keep
both hands on what you are actually doing.

:::note
This drives an **existing** pump through its remote port. It is not a pump — the
peristaltic head, the motor and the tubing are the pump you already own. Check
your pump has a DB25 remote connector before building this.
:::

## What it does

- **Set a dose** on the rotary encoder, read it off the seven-segment display.
- **Trigger it** from either of two footswitches — one per hand's worth of work,
  or one for dose and one for continuous.
- **Run the pump forward or in reverse** through the relay pair, for priming and
  clearing lines.
- **A 24-LED NeoPixel ring** shows progress through a dose as it runs, so you can
  see where you are without looking at the display.
- **Reads the pump's own feedback line** through a voltage sensor, rather than
  assuming the pump did what it was told.

## Wiring

Taken from the firmware's pin definitions, which are the authority here.

| Function | Arduino pin | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Encoder DT | **D2** | Rotary encoder |
| Encoder CLK | **D3** | Rotary encoder |
| Encoder switch | **D10** | Rotary encoder SW |
| Pump start | **D4** | DB25 **pin 15** |
| NeoPixel data | **D5** | 24-LED ring |
| Pump speed set | **D6** | Voltage booster → DB25 **pin 1** |
| Display CLK | **D7** | MAX7219 |
| Display CS | **D8** | MAX7219 |
| Display DIN | **D9** | MAX7219 |
| Pump direction | **D13** | DB25 **pin 16** |
| Relay IN1 | **A4** | 2-channel relay |
| Relay IN2 | **A5** | 2-channel relay |
| Left footswitch | **A0** | Footswitch |
| Right footswitch | **A1** | Footswitch |
| Pump feedback | **A3** | Voltage sensor |

:::caution
The display pins are the one place the source will mislead you. The comment above
the constructor in the sketch reads *"7 to DIN, 4 to CS, 5 to CLK"*, but the
constructor itself is `DigitLedDisplay(9, 8, 7)` — DIN 9, CS 8, CLK 7. **The
constructor is what runs.** The comment is left over from an earlier revision.
:::

## Building the case

The case is laser-cut acrylic, not printed — five files in the download section.
`pump_controller_housing_v4` is the current housing; `front_final`, `swirl_final`
and `Sides` are the panels that go with it.

<Steps>

1. **Cut the panels.** Both DXF and SVG are provided — use whichever your cutter
   is happier with. **Check the scale on import**, because some laser software
   silently re-scales an imported DXF.

2. **Test-fit before you glue.** Acrylic kerf varies by machine and thickness; the
   finger joints are cut for a particular kerf and may need a pass with a file.

3. **Mount the panel hardware** — encoder, display, LED ring, footswitch jacks —
   into the front panel while it is still flat and reachable.

4. **Wire it up** to the table above, then solder the DB25 last: it is the
   connector you least want to redo inside a closed box.

</Steps>

## Firmware

The sketch is `BRUHpumpcontrollerV6.ino`, in the download section below. It needs
two Arduino libraries:

- **Adafruit NeoPixel** — for the ring
- **DigitLedDisplay** — for the MAX7219

Both install from the Arduino IDE's Library Manager. Keep the `.ino` inside a
folder of the same name, which is how the IDE expects to find a sketch.

There is a `#define DEBUG` at the top — leave it in while you are bringing the
board up and it will report over serial what it thinks each input is doing.
Comment it out when the box is closed.

## Calibration

Volume per revolution depends on your tubing bore and your pump head, so the dose
numbers mean nothing until you calibrate. Pump into a tared vessel on a balance,
weigh what came out, and adjust until the display agrees with the scale. Re-do it
whenever the tubing changes — peristaltic tubing takes a set as it ages, and an
old line delivers less per turn than a new one.
