Press the two panels flat, drop your keys between them, let go, and the spring
shuts them and carries the keys about 60 mm back up to where your hand already
is. There is no bowl to fish around in and no hook to aim for — the mechanism
*is* the holder. Four printed parts, two pins and one spring.

:::note
**The CAD is the mechanism, and it is missing the two things that hold the
mechanism up.** Everything below is measured off `popup_key_holder_v7.step` and
it is a complete, coherent linkage: base, cam slider, two arms, two hinge pins.
But the pins float in space. Nothing in the file connects them to the base
plate — there are no bearing blocks, no uprights, no ears — and they sit 51 mm
above it. There is no latch and no spring in the model either.

So this page documents the linkage properly, gives you every dimension you need
to draw the two uprights it wants, and specifies the spring the geometry asks
for. It is not yet a print-it-and-build-it guide, which is why it still carries
the unfinished flag.
:::

## How it works

A 5 mm post stands out of a flat base plate. A 30 mm dia slider rides up and
down that post, and a spring under the slider pushes it to the top. Two arms
pivot on pins either side of the post, and each arm has a short foot reaching
inward whose rounded tip sits captive in the waist of the slider — the groove
between the slider's 18 mm collar and the cove under its 30 mm barrel.

That is the whole machine. The slider's height sets the angle of both arms, and
because both feet sit in the same groove the two panels are locked to each other
and can only move together.

| The slider | The arms |
|---|---|
| At the top of its travel | Shut — the two panels stand at 63° and meet in a mitre at the apex |
| 20.5 mm down | Flat — the panels lay right back |
| Anywhere between | Somewhere between, symmetrically |

The upper stop is the mitre itself: the panel tips meet and the spring cannot
push the slider any further. That means the mechanism sets its own rest position
and needs no adjustment.

## What's in the CAD

Every number here is measured off `popup_key_holder_v7.step`. Assembled and
shut, the thing is **80 × 80 mm on the table and 119.4 mm tall**.

### Base and post

One printed body. An 80 × 80 × 2.4 mm plate, square, no fillets and no mounting
holes anywhere in it. Out of the middle rises a **5 mm dia post, 95.6 mm of
parallel length**, finished with a **10 mm dia × 2.2 mm head** — so 97.8 mm of
post above the plate. Model volume 17.4 cm³.

### Slider

A turned part, 30 mm dia and 54.0 mm long, model volume 25.4 cm³. Measured from
the collar face:

| From the collar face | Feature |
|---|---|
| 0 → 2.9 mm | 18 mm dia collar |
| 2.9 → 5.9 mm | R3 fillet, 18 mm down to 9 mm |
| 5.9 → 9.0 mm | 9 mm dia neck |
| 9.0 → 13.0 mm | curved cove, 9 mm back out to 30 mm |
| 13.0 → 51.0 mm | 30 mm dia barrel |
| 51.0 → 54.0 mm | R3 roll-over on the far end |

Bored from the same face: **9 mm dia for 3.8 mm**, then **6 mm dia for
30.2 mm**, then **18.9 mm dia for the last 20 mm**. The 6 mm bore is what runs
on the post — 1 mm of diametral clearance, which is right for a printed
bearing. The 9 mm counterbore is the spring seat, and the 18.9 mm pocket at the
far end is clearance so the post's head can sink into the slider.

### Arms

Two of them, and they are the reason this prints as well as it does: each arm is
a flat 2D profile extruded **80 mm** wide, **2.4 mm thick throughout**, with no
feature anywhere across the width except the pivot bore. Profile envelope
33.8 × 73.6 mm, model volume about 25 cm³ each.

| Feature | Dimension |
|---|---|
| Pivot bore | 5.0 mm dia, straight through all 80 mm |
| Pivot to panel tip | 72.3 mm |
| Pivot to foot tip | 27.8 mm |
| Foot tip | 2.4 mm thick, R1 on both corners |
| Panel face | 80 × 73.4 mm flat |

The two arms are near-mirrors rather than exact ones — one has an R4.5 corner on
the heel where the other has R6. It changes nothing about how it works, but
print both of them rather than printing one twice and flipping it.

### Pins

**3 mm dia × 80 mm**, one per arm — exactly as wide as an arm, so they finish
flush at both ends. The two pivot axes are **67.0 mm apart** and sit **51.3 mm
above the top face of the base plate**. Those three numbers are the ones you
need to draw the uprights the model doesn't have.

### The spring

Not in the file. The geometry specifies it anyway: it stands on the base plate,
passes over the 5 mm post, and seats in the 9 mm counterbore in the underside of
the slider. At rest that gap is **40.5 mm**, and the slider has to travel
20.5 mm down to lay the panels flat.

So: **9 mm outside diameter** so it registers in the counterbore, **over 5 mm
inside** so it clears the post, **around 45 mm free length** so it is preloaded
at 40.5 mm, and it must still compress to 20 mm without going solid. A 0.8 mm
wire spring in that envelope is about right.

## Before you slice it

:::caution
**The STL is the whole assembly in one file, in the assembled position.** Send
`popup_key_holder_v7.stl` straight to a slicer and you get seven separate solids
floating in mid-air — base, slider, two arms, two pins and one extra — 119 mm
tall, most of it unsupported. It is a viewer file, not a print file.
:::

Split it first. Every slicer has it: *Split to objects* in PrusaSlicer and
Bambu Studio, *Split to parts* in Cura. Then delete the two 3 mm pins, which you
are buying in steel, and lay the four remaining parts flat.

There is also a **stray body** in the export. A thin slat, 1.95 × 16.3 × 85 mm,
leaning against the outside of one arm's heel with no mirror on the other side
and nothing to attach to. It is the only thing in the file that breaks the
symmetry, and it is what pushes the raw STL's bounding box out to 84 × 85 mm.
Delete it after the split, or open the STEP and drop `Body10` before you export.

## Print settings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Layer height | 0.2 mm |
| Walls / perimeters | 3 — that is 2.4 mm on a 0.4 mm nozzle, so the arms come out solid |
| Infill | 25%, and only the base plate and the slider have any |
| Supports | None |
| Material | PETG for the arms and slider, PLA+ for the base |

| Part | On the bed | Height |
|---|---|---|
| Base and post | Plate down, post up | 100.2 mm |
| Slider | The end the big pocket opens through | 54.0 mm |
| Arm ×2 | A flat panel face down, heel and foot standing up | 33 mm |

**The arms are the easy ones.** Lay a panel face on the bed and you get an
80 × 73 mm flat footprint, the foot stands off it at 66° and supports itself,
and there is not an overhang anywhere in the part. The pivot bore then runs
horizontally, which means it prints with a drooping crown and comes out
egg-shaped — that is fine, it is a hole you were going to ream anyway.

**Stand the slider on the end with the 18.9 mm pocket in it.** That end is a
24 mm flat with a 3 mm roll-over around it, so the first millimetre or so comes
out rough; run a brim and forget about it. The 6 mm bore then prints vertically
and stays round, which is the fit that matters most on the whole model. The
pocket's ceiling bridges 6.5 mm and needs nothing.

**The post is the part that will fail.** 5 mm dia and 96 mm tall is a spindly
column to print and a spindly column to live with. Slow the outer wall right
down, make sure part cooling is on it, and print it on its own so each layer has
time to set. If it snaps — and it is the thing that snaps — drill the plate out
and glue in a length of steel rod instead.

## Assembly

<Steps>

1. **Draw and print the two uprights.** This is the part the folder does not
   have. They need to carry a 3 mm (or 5 mm) pin **67.0 mm apart and 51.3 mm
   above the top face of the base plate**, and they have to stay outside the
   80 mm the arms sweep through. Two blocks on the plate is the obvious answer;
   a U-shaped frame the plate drops into is the better one, because it puts the
   two bearings on the same part and they cannot drift apart.

2. **Ream every bore.** A 1/4 in (6.35 mm) bit through the slider's 6 mm bore —
   that fit has to be free, not snug, because the spring is what has to move it.
   The pivot bores get whatever matches the pin you settled on. Turn every bit
   by hand; a drill on a trigger grabs printed plastic and spirals it out.

3. **Check the slider runs.** Drop it on the post dry, no spring, no arms. It
   should fall down the post under its own weight and rattle a little. If it
   binds anywhere, ream again — every bit of friction here comes straight off
   how hard it snaps shut.

4. **Cut two 80 mm pins** and dress the ends. They finish flush with the arms,
   so anything proud is a burr you will feel later.

5. **Hang the arms on the slider first, then pin them.** Get both foot tips into
   the waist of the slider — the groove between the collar and the cove — before
   you push either pin home. Trying to fit a foot into that groove with the arm
   already pinned means levering on a 2.4 mm section.

6. **Fit the spring last**, between the base plate and the counterbore in the
   underside of the slider, and check the panels close all the way to the mitre
   at the apex. If they stop short, the spring is going solid before the slider
   reaches the top and you need a longer one or thinner wire.

7. **A drop of CA on each pin end** once everything swings freely, and four
   bumpers on the bottom.

</Steps>

## Two things that will bite you

### The bore and the pin disagree

The pivot bore in each arm is drawn at **5.0 mm**. The pins drawn through them
are **3.0 mm**. That is a millimetre of slop per side, and in a linkage where
both arms are supposed to be locked to each other through one slider, slop is
the difference between a mechanism and a rattle.

Two honest ways out, and neither is hard. Use **5 mm rod** and the model is
correct as drawn — the bore is nominal, so it will need reaming to actually take
it, which is what you would do anyway. Or use the **3 mm stainless** and open
the STEP first, taking the two bores down to 3.2 mm. Pick before you print the
arms; it is not a fix you can apply afterwards.

### It has no latch

The spring holds the panels shut and nothing else does. Push against the peak
and it opens, which is exactly what you want when you are putting keys in and
exactly what you do not want when somebody knocks the table. There is nothing in
the model for this — no magnet pocket, no detent, no over-centre. If you want it
to stay shut, that is a feature you are adding, and the easiest place for it is
a pair of small magnets in the mitred tips where the panels already meet.
