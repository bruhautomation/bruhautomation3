The S20 is a plug and a relay and nothing else — no power monitoring, no
metering chip. That makes it the right first ESPHome flash: four pads, a short
config, and a device that stops phoning home the moment it reboots.

If you want to know how much the thing plugged into it is drawing, build the
[Sonoff S31](/projects/sonoff-s31/) instead — same flashing procedure, real
metering inside.

## What you need

<AffiliateDisclosure />

- [Sonoff S20 smart plug](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Sonoff+S20+smart+plug&tag=brau01-20) — check the socket type matches your country
- [USB to serial adapter (3.3 V)](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=CP2102+USB+to+TTL+serial+adapter+3.3V&tag=brau01-20)
- [Jumper wires or a pogo-pin jig](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dupont+jumper+wires+female&tag=brau01-20)
- A small Phillips screwdriver

:::danger
Unplug it from the wall before opening it, and never connect the serial adapter
while it is in a socket. Flash on the bench, reassemble, then plug it in.
:::

## Flashing

<Steps>

1. **Open the case** — one screw under the label, then release the clips.

2. **Find the four-pad header** on the board: `3V3`, `RX`, `TX`, `GND`. On some
   revisions it is unpopulated and you will need to solder in a header or hold
   pogo pins against it.

3. **Wire the adapter with the data lines crossed** — adapter TX to board RX,
   adapter RX to board TX, plus 3V3 and GND.

4. **Hold the button down as you connect USB.** That is what selects flash mode.

5. **Flash the config below**, then reassemble. Everything after this is over WiFi.

</Steps>

## What the config gives you

An `esp01_1m` board — 1 MB flash, which is why the config stays small.

| Function | GPIO | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Relay | **GPIO12** | The switch entity in Home Assistant |
| Status LED | **GPIO13** | Inverted, driven as a PWM light |
| Button | **GPIO0** | Inverted; also the flash-mode button |

In Home Assistant you get a switch, the LED as a dimmable light, WiFi signal and
uptime. The physical button toggles the relay locally, so the plug still works
if Home Assistant is down — which is most of the reason to run it locally.

## Troubleshooting

**Nothing happens on flash.** The button must be held *as* power arrives. Also
check TX/RX aren't straight-through — crossed is correct.

**It flashes but never joins WiFi.** 2.4 GHz only; the ESP8266 cannot see a 5 GHz
network. If your router publishes both under one name, that is usually the cause.

**The relay clicks but the socket is dead.** That is a mains-side fault, not a
firmware one. Stop and check your work before plugging anything else in.
