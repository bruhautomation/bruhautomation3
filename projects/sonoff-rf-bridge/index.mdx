There is a whole world of $3 sensors on 433 MHz — door contacts, PIRs, water
leak pucks, four-button keyfobs — that predate WiFi and will outlast it on a
coin cell. The RF Bridge is the translator: it listens on 433 MHz, and ESPHome
turns what it hears into Home Assistant events.

This is the cheapest way to add a dozen door sensors to a house, and the battery
life is measured in years rather than months.

## What you need

- [Sonoff RF Bridge 433](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Sonoff+RF+Bridge+433MHz&tag=brau01-20)
- [USB to serial adapter (3.3 V)](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=CP2102+USB+to+TTL+serial+adapter+3.3V&tag=brau01-20)
- [433 MHz sensors and remotes](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=433MHz+door+sensor+PIR+remote+kit&tag=brau01-20) — whatever you want it to hear
- A small Phillips screwdriver

:::note
Buy the **433 MHz** version. There is a 315 MHz variant sold alongside it, and
the two cannot hear each other. Check what your sensors use before ordering.
:::

## Flashing

<Steps>

1. **Open the case** — four screws in the base.

2. **Find the four-pin header** near the ESP8266: `3V3`, `RX`, `TX`, `GND`. On
   most units it is unpopulated, so solder in a header or hold pogo pins.

3. **Wire the adapter with the data lines crossed** — adapter TX to board RX,
   adapter RX to board TX, plus 3V3 and GND.

4. **Bridge the `GPIO0` pad to ground** as you apply power, to select flash mode.
   Some revisions expose a button for this instead.

5. **Flash the config below.** Everything after this is over WiFi.

</Steps>

## How it works

Unlike the S20 and S31, the interesting pins here are **not** GPIO. The bridge
carries a separate RF chip that does the demodulating, and it talks to the
ESP8266 over a serial link:

| Function | Pin | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| RF module TX | **GPIO1** | ESP transmits codes to the RF chip |
| RF module RX | **GPIO3** | ESP receives decoded codes |

Because GPIO1 and GPIO3 *are* the hardware UART, the config runs the ESPHome
logger with its own UART disabled. If you turn serial logging back on, the
bridge stops hearing anything — the log and the RF chip are fighting over the
same two pins.

## Pairing a sensor

<Steps>

1. Open the ESPHome log for the bridge.

2. Trigger the sensor — open the door, wave at the PIR, press the fob.

3. A received code appears in the log. **Write it down**; that number is the
   sensor's identity.

4. Add a `binary_sensor` to the config matching that code, give it a name and a
   `device_class`, and reflash.

</Steps>

Repeat per sensor. The codes are fixed per device, so a sensor keeps its identity
across battery changes.

:::caution
433 MHz sensors of this type are not encrypted or authenticated — anything in
range can transmit the same code. They are fine for "the laundry door opened" and
a bad idea as the only thing standing between someone and your alarm being armed.
:::

## Troubleshooting

**No codes ever appear.** Usually the wrong band — a 315 MHz sensor against a
433 MHz bridge. Otherwise check the logger's UART is disabled in the config.

**Codes appear but the sensor is unreliable at range.** The onboard antenna is a
PCB trace. Moving the bridge more central in the house beats anything else you
can do.

**One sensor triggers another's automation.** Two devices sharing a code — common
with cheap multipacks programmed at the factory. Re-pair one, or use a different
brand for that spot.
