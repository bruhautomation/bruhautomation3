A pan-tilt bracket, two servos and a red laser diode, driven by an ESP32 so
Home Assistant can run the cat around the living room at 3pm on a Tuesday while
nobody is home. Everything on the shelf that does this is a battery toy with a
fixed sweep the cat learns in a week; the point of building it is that the
pattern comes from an automation, and an automation can be random.

:::note
**This is at concept stage.** The two images below are renders, not photographs —
there are no STLs, no CAD and no firmware in the folder yet. What is real is the
parts list: every part below is a specific thing that exists, and the build
described is the one those parts make. Treat it as a shopping list and a set of
design decisions already taken, not a build guide.
:::

![Concept render of the pan-tilt laser turret, aimed across a table](./images/laser-turret-concept-alt.png)

While we are being honest about the renders: a 5 mW diode will not light that
candle, will not cut anything, and will not leave a mark on a wall. It makes a
dot. That is the entire job.

## Parts

- [ESP32 DevKit, 38-pin](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07QCP2451?tag=brau01-20) — 2-pack. The ESP32's hardware LEDC timers give clean servo PWM; an ESP8266 bit-bangs it and the jitter shows up as twitch
- [650 nm 5 mW red laser diode module](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07L4225TM?tag=brau01-20) — 2-pack, so you have a spare. The variant here is the cross-line head, which throws an X rather than a dot; the cat does not care, but pick the dot version if you want the classic
- [Pan/tilt servo bracket](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PQ12TXS?tag=brau01-20) — 2 sets. This is the part that decides the whole build: it is cut for standard-size servos, so that is what goes in it
- [MG996R metal-gear servos](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08CH2SJLR?tag=brau01-20) ×2 — 6-pack. Standard size, metal gears, and the exact frame the bracket above is drilled for
- [MOSFET trigger module](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07XJSRY6B?tag=brau01-20) — 10-pack. Switches the laser's ground from a 3.3 V GPIO
- [12 V 4 A power supply](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07H493GHX?tag=brau01-20) — one barrel jack in, everything derived from it
- [20 A CC/CV buck converter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07Y7YB14L?tag=brau01-20) — 2-pack. Set to 6.0 V, feeds the servos
- [MP1495 buck module](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CDWTQG9B?tag=brau01-20) — 5-pack. Set to 5.0 V, feeds the ESP32 and the laser
- [Dupont jumper assortment](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077X99KX1?tag=brau01-20) and [24 AWG silicone wire](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0881H2LR5?tag=brau01-20) — servo leads are short and you will need to extend them
- [PLA+ filament](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CJ2LB5FZ?tag=brau01-20) for the base, plus [M3 heat-set inserts](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08BCRZZS3?tag=brau01-20) and an [M3 screw assortment](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BLCFZF14?tag=brau01-20) to bolt the bracket down
- [Self-adhesive rubber bumpers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B088HGQSZT?tag=brau01-20) — feet, so a fast pan does not walk the turret off the shelf

Optional, if you would rather it only ran when the cat is actually in the room
than on a blind schedule: an [HC-SR501 PIR](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00FDPO9B8?tag=brau01-20)
on a spare input, or any presence sensor Home Assistant already has.

A [PCA9685 servo driver](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B082QT9D5F?tag=brau01-20) is
worth having in the drawer but is not needed here — it earns its place past about
four servos, and this is two.

## Power

Two servos and a laser is not a USB-powered project. An MG996R pulls well over an
amp the instant it changes direction, and if it is taking that out of the ESP32's
regulator the board browns out mid-sweep and reboots. The board reboots, the servos
snap to whatever the config's default is, and you have just flung a laser at the
ceiling.

One 12 V brick, two buck converters off the same rail:

- **6.0 V** from the CC/CV module → the two servos' red wires. MG996Rs run 4.8–7.2 V
  and are noticeably stronger and quicker at 6 than at 5.
- **5.0 V** from the MP1495 → the ESP32's 5V pin and the laser module.

Deriving both from one supply means the grounds are common by construction, which
is the failure everybody hits when they run the servos off a separate wall wart and
wonder why the signal is garbage.

:::caution
Set both bucks with a multimeter across the output terminals **before** anything is
connected to them. These modules ship with the trim pot at an arbitrary position,
and the first thing a 12 V output does to an MG996R is destroy it.
:::

## Wiring

There is no firmware in this folder, so there are no authoritative pin numbers to
publish. What follows is the electrical layout — the GPIO choices are yours to make
in the config.

| From | To | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 12 V brick + | Input of both bucks | Common 12 V rail |
| CC/CV buck out (6.0 V) | Servo red ×2 | Servos only — never the ESP32 |
| MP1495 out (5.0 V) | ESP32 5V pin, laser module + | |
| All grounds | Tied together | Bucks, servos, laser, ESP32, MOSFET module |
| Pan servo signal (orange) | An ESP32 GPIO | |
| Tilt servo signal (orange) | A second GPIO | |
| MOSFET module trigger in | A third GPIO | Laser on/off |
| Laser module − | MOSFET module switched output | The module switches the low side |

Do not hang the laser straight off a GPIO. It is only tens of milliamps, which is
close enough to the pin's limit to work on the bench and fail in a box in July.

Some pins to avoid when you pick them: GPIO 0, 2, 12 and 15 are strapping pins and
a servo held at the wrong level through a reset will stop the board booting, and
GPIO 34–39 are input-only, so nothing that has to *drive* anything goes there.

## Design notes

The bracket is the constraint everything else follows from. It is drilled for
standard-size servos, which is why the parts list says MG996R rather than a micro
servo — a 9 g servo will not bolt into it without new holes. The trade is noise:
MG996Rs hunt around their setpoint and buzz when they are holding still, which is
audible across a quiet living room and is exactly the kind of thing a cat learns to
ignore. ESPHome's `servo` component has an `auto_detach_time`, and setting it means
the PWM stops once the turret has arrived and the whole thing goes silent between
moves. Use it.

Centre both servos before you fit the horns. Drive each one to its mid position,
*then* screw the arm on square. Fit the horns first and you will find one axis has
30° of travel one way and 150° the other, and no amount of config fixes it.

![Concept render of the turret and its target](./images/laser-turret-concept.png)

The base is the part that does not exist yet, and it has one real requirement: mass.
A pan servo snapping 90° will drag a light printed puck across a table. Print it with
high infill, or make it big enough to sit a weight inside, and put rubber feet under
it. Keep the pan travel under a full turn so the servo leads can just flex rather
than needing a slip ring.

## Laser safety

:::danger
A 650 nm 5 mW module is Class 3R. It will not burn anything, but it can damage an
eye — a cat's or yours — and a servo makes it point wherever the firmware says,
which includes at a face at the exact moment a config reload lands.

Solve it with geometry, not software. Mount the turret low, aim it down, and put a
**physical** stop on the tilt axis so the beam cannot rise above the skirting board
at the far wall. Software limits are worth setting too, but they are the second line
of defence: a reboot happens before the config does.
:::

Never chase the cat's face with it, and never let a guest hold the phone.

## The cat part

A laser is the one toy a cat can never catch, and a session that ends with the dot
just switching off leaves an animal wound up with nothing to show for it. Land the
dot on a real toy or a treat and turn it off there. That is one line at the end of
the automation and it is the difference between a good toy and an annoying one.

Five minutes is plenty. The novelty is in it being unpredictable, which means the
automation's random-interval, random-position version beats any fixed sweep — and it
is also the reason this is worth building instead of buying.
