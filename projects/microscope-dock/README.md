# Microscope Dock

A printed dock that clamps to a microscope eyepiece and holds a phone on the
optical axis, so what you saw down the tube gets photographed instead of
described.

**Build guide:** [bruhautomation.com/projects/microscope-dock](https://bruhautomation.com/projects/microscope-dock/)

## Hardware

- A collar in PETG that clamps the eyepiece **barrel**, not the rubber eyecup —
  a clamp lives under constant load and PLA creeps under one
- A cradle in PLA+ that places the phone's main camera lens on the optical axis
- TPU band inside the collar and TPU or rubber pads in the cradle, so nothing
  rigid touches the optics or the phone
- M3 heat-set inserts ×2 and a knurled M3 thumbscrew — gloved hands and hex keys
  don't mix
- An articulating arm clamped to the bench, carrying the phone's weight through
  a 1/4 in-20 screw. The collar holds alignment; it should never hold load

Measure the eyepiece barrel with calipers first — the microscope's 23.2 mm or
30 mm tube bore is not the diameter you are clamping. Print a 20 mm test ring
before the full collar.

If the scope has a trinocular photo port, put a C-mount camera on it instead.
This is for the binocular scope nobody is going to buy a camera for.

Not autoclavable. Wipe with 70% ethanol.
