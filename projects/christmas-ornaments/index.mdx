An ornament is the smallest project a 3D printer is good for: an hour on the
bed, a few grams of filament, and no reason to stop at one. These are the house's
own hardware hung on the tree — the shapes that live on the shelves and behind
the wall plates the rest of the year — printed as hollow shells with somewhere
inside for a light to go.

The only decision that changes the build is what that light is. A flameless tea
light drops in, costs nothing and needs no wiring. A single addressable pixel
pressed into the shell puts the ornament on the same string as the tree. An ESP
inside makes it its own device in Home Assistant, which is the most work for the
least visible difference — do that one on the ornament that sits at eye level.

## Parts

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Filament first, because on this project the filament *is* the design. Nothing
here gets painted.

- [Glow-in-the-dark PLA](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FB3LV36D?tag=brau01-20) — the one filament that makes an ornament work with no electronics in it at all. This spool glows blue; the colour of the glow is the colour you get, so pick it before you model anything
- [Matte white PLA](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B089S1HB8K?tag=brau01-20) — the shell material for anything lit from inside
- [Clear PLA](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07ZNG4L9P?tag=brau01-20) — icicles, and anything meant to sparkle rather than glow
- [Silk tri-colour PLA](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BN5Z2798?tag=brau01-20) — pink, blue and silver co-extruded on one strand, so the colour shifts up the ornament with no work from you
- [Colour-changing gradient PLA](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BV69PNHY?tag=brau01-20) — black through to green over the spool
- [PLA+ in black](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CJ2LB5FZ?tag=brau01-20) — 4 kg, for caps, backs and the second colour of a two-tone print
- [Hardened steel 0.4 mm nozzle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08VNNZH37?tag=brau01-20) — a 20-pack, and you want one fitted before the glow filament goes in

Then pick one of the three ways to light it.

- [Flameless LED tea lights](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BQJ5QZ8J?tag=brau01-20) — 24 in a pack, warm white, flickering. The whole lighting system for a dozen ornaments, for the price of one pixel ring
- [CR2032 cells](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DKXJCMSZ?tag=brau01-20) — 50-pack; most flameless tea lights take one, and a fresh cell is cheaper than a new tea light
- [12 V WS2811 12 mm bullet pixels](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0922DZ4CC?tag=brau01-20) — 100 diffused nodes on a 10 m string. One node per ornament, the string does the rest
- [12 V 10 A supply with an 8-way splitter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B018G3ABWY?tag=brau01-20) — what feeds a full bullet-pixel string. A wall wart will brown out halfway down the tree
- [WS2812 12-pixel ring](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C7CFQXY3?tag=brau01-20) — 2-pack, for an ornament with its own controller
- [WS2812B strip, 5 V, 30 LEDs/m](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B019HAAVLQ?tag=brau01-20) — cut single pixels off this when a ring is more light than the shape needs
- [WS2812 8-LED board](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07KD6ZB88?tag=brau01-20) — a rigid bar rather than a strip, easier to solder to
- [Wemos D1 mini](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01N3P763C?tag=brau01-20) — the ESP that actually fits inside an ornament
- [JST-SM 3-pin pigtails](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CGPV3FMG?tag=brau01-20) — so an ornament unplugs from the string instead of being cut off it
- [24 AWG silicone wire](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0881H2LR5?tag=brau01-20) — 10 colours; stays flexible, which matters on a lead that gets bent every December

And the bits that hold it together.

- [6 × 2 mm neodymium magnets](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B075RZ82HP?tag=brau01-20) — 120-pack, so the shell opens to change a cell instead of being glued shut
- [CA glue with activator](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0849YVVSL?tag=brau01-20) — for the ornaments that *are* glued shut
- [365 nm UV torch](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CNSLGFWG?tag=brau01-20) — charges a glow-filament ornament in seconds instead of leaving it under a lamp
- [Ornament hooks](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09D9M97QB?tag=brau01-20) — the one thing here worth buying blind

## Print settings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Layer height | 0.2 mm |
| Walls | 2 |
| Infill | 0–10% |
| Supports | None |
| Material | PLA |
| Orientation | Flat face on the bed, seam to the back |

Two walls, because every wall you add is light you don't get back. A shell meant
to glow is a bowl: no infill, no top layers, and the cap is a separate part. It
prints faster than a solid one too.

**Matte white is the diffuser, not clear.** Clear PLA prints frosty and passes a
lot of light, but you can still see the LED through it as a hard bright dot.
Matte white turns a point source into an even glow, which is the whole effect
you are after. Keep the clear filament for icicles and shapes that catch the
tree's own lights.

**Glow filament is abrasive.** It is loaded with strontium aluminate, and a brass
nozzle comes out measurably wider after a spool of it. Fit the hardened nozzle
before you start and leave it in — the same nozzle handles carbon-fibre filament
later.

Glow also needs charging. A few seconds under the 365 nm torch and the ornament
holds its glow long after the room lights go off — no supply, no controller, and
nothing to unplug in January.

For two-tone shapes — a logo or a face in one colour on a body in another —
either run them on a multi-material printer (the [Bambu Lab A1
Combo](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D17V4SKM?tag=brau01-20) ships with the AMS
lite, which is four colours in one print) or put a colour change at the layer
where the inlay starts. The colour change costs nothing and works on any
printer, as long as the second colour only appears on the top face and never
comes back.

:::caution
Do not hang a PLA ornament against an old incandescent light string. PLA softens
around 60 °C, and a C7 or C9 bulb resting on a shell will leave a dent in it by
New Year. Modern LED strings run cool enough that it stops being a problem.
:::

## Wiring

Only the ESP version has any. A ring, a board, three wires.

| Ring pin | D1 mini | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| DIN | Any free GPIO | Not D3, D4 or D8 — the ESP8266 samples those at reset, and a ring on one can stop it booting. Name whichever you pick in the config; keep the lead short |
| 5V | **5V** | Off the board's 5 V pin, which is USB power passed through. Not 3V3 |
| GND | GND | Common ground, and it has to be common if the ring is powered separately |

Twelve WS2812s at full white pull the better part of an amp, which is more than a
thin micro-USB cable and a D1 mini's traces want to carry. Cap the brightness in
the config at something like a third — you are lighting an ornament the size of a
fist, not a room, and it will still be the brightest thing on the tree.

The bullet-pixel route has no wiring at all beyond the string's own connectors:
each 12 mm node presses into a 12 mm hole in the shell, and the ornaments become
pixels on whatever controller already drives the tree.

## Assembly

<Steps>

1. **Print one before you print twelve.** The fit of the light in the shell is
   the only thing that can be wrong, and it is much cheaper to find out on the
   first one.

2. **Dry-fit the light.** A tea light should drop in and sit flat; a bullet pixel
   should push into its hole and stay there without glue. If either is tight,
   open the hole with a needle file rather than reprinting — printed holes come
   out slightly undersized on almost every machine.

3. **Set the magnets, if the shell opens.** Two in the cap, two in the body,
   glued in with CA. **Check the polarity of all four before the glue goes off** —
   one magnet in backwards and the cap pushes itself off the ornament every time
   you close it.

4. **Wire it, if there is anything to wire.** Solder to the ring before it goes in
   the shell, not after, and put a JST pigtail on the lead so the ornament can
   come off the string in January without cutting anything.

5. **Close it up.** CA with activator on the seam for the sealed ones — it grabs
   instantly, so line the halves up first and only then hit it with the spray.

6. **Thread the hanger** and hang it high enough that the dog's tail doesn't
   reach it.

</Steps>
