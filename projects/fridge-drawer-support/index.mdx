Crisper drawers drop. The ledge they ride on wears, the drawer front sags, and
you get that scrape on the way in and a gap at the top on the way shut. This is
the fix: a flat printed bar that sits on the ledge and puts the drawer back where
it was. It takes ten grams of filament and no fasteners at all.

<ImagePlaceholder alt="A printed white bar sitting on the rail ledge inside a refrigerator, with the crisper drawer pulled halfway out">
  Hero shot — the bar in place on the ledge with the drawer pulled halfway out,
  so you can see both the shim and the drawer it carries.
</ImagePlaceholder>

## What the part actually is

Worth being precise, because "bracket" undersells how simple it is. The model is
a bar **228 mm long, 18 mm wide and 2 mm thick**. The bottom is flat — the full
228 × 18 rectangle, sitting on the ledge. The whole top perimeter is rolled over
with a 2 mm radius, which because the bar is only 2 mm thick means the roll runs
all the way from the flat top down to the plate. There is no sharp shoulder
anywhere on the upper face, so the drawer slides on and off it without catching
on an edge. There are no holes and no fasteners.

The 2 mm thickness is the specification here. That is how much lift you get.

## Parts

<AffiliateDisclosure />

- [PETG filament, 1.75 mm](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00ZAUR6M0?tag=brau01-20) — about 10 g per bar, so a 1 kg spool covers every fridge you will ever own
- [3M VHB double-sided tape, 1/2 in](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01BT0A6MG?tag=brau01-20) — 12.7 mm wide, which lands neatly down the middle of an 18 mm bar
- [Digital caliper](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000NEA0P8?tag=brau01-20) — the one tool this project genuinely needs, because 228 mm is one particular fridge and not yours
- [Wet/dry sandpaper, assorted grit](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07R64PZ8N?tag=brau01-20) — for the bottom edge and for taking the bar down to length
- [Deburring tool](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09ND84SXF?tag=brau01-20) — optional, but it kills an elephant's foot in one pass where sandpaper takes five minutes

:::note
Print it in PETG. A fridge is cold and wet, and this is a 2 mm strip you have to
flex slightly to get past the drawer stops. Cold PLA snaps doing that. PETG bends
and comes back, and it does not care about condensation. If all you have is
[PLA+](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CJ2LB5FZ?tag=brau01-20) it will work — just
fit it gently, and expect to reprint it the first time you are rough with the
drawer.
:::

## Measure before you print

The bar is drawn for one drawer. Pull yours all the way out and take three
numbers with the caliper:

- **How long the ledge run is.** That sets the 228 mm.
- **How wide the ledge is.** That sets the 18 mm. Narrower is fine; wider than
  the ledge and the drawer rides on an unsupported overhang.
- **How far the drawer has dropped.** That sets the 2 mm.

The three are independent, so do not uniform-scale the STL — scaling it to fit
the length changes the lift and the width along with it. Change the length in
the slicer's X axis only, or open `fridge_drawer_support_v1.step` and edit it
properly. If you need more than 2 mm of lift, raise the thickness in CAD and
raise the bullnose radius with it: the radius equals the thickness by design,
and leaving it at 2 mm on a thicker bar gives you back the sharp shoulder the
part exists to avoid.

The lazy version works too. Print it at 228 mm, mark where it needs to end, and
take it down with the sandpaper — 2 mm of PETG gives up quickly.

## Print settings

Flat face down, rolled face up, exactly as exported. Nothing overhangs.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Layer height | 0.15 mm |
| Walls | 4 or more |
| Infill | 100% |
| Supports | None |
| Brim | Yes |
| Material | PETG |
| Orientation | Flat, as exported |

0.15 mm rather than 0.2 mm because at 2 mm total height the bullnose is only ten
or thirteen layers of curve, and the difference between those two numbers is
visible as stepping on the roll. Run it at 0.2 mm if you would rather have the
time back; it fits the same.

Solid infill is not optional. At 2 mm thick the top and bottom shells almost meet
anyway, and a hollow bar with a 15% gyroid inside crushes under the drawer and
stops lifting it. The perimeters are the part that matters — they run the full
228 mm, which is the direction the load pulls, so more walls is strictly better
until the cross-section is solid.

:::caution
**228 mm does not fit an Ender 3's 220 × 220 bed along an axis.** Rotate the bar
45° on the plate and the diagonal swallows it easily. On a 235 mm plate it fits
straight with about 3 mm at each end — turn the skirt off, or it prints into the
bed clips.
:::

Two other things about a long thin part. It will curl at the ends if you let it,
which is what the brim is for. And PETG on smooth PEI bonds hard enough to take
the sheet with it — print this on the textured side.

## Fitting it

<Steps>

1. **Print two.** A drawer runs on a ledge at each side, and lifting one side
   only racks it — it will bind worse than the sag did. The part is symmetric,
   so there is no left and right; print the same file twice.

2. **Take the drawer and the shelf out and let them warm up.** VHB will not bond
   to a cold, damp surface, and the inside of a fridge is exactly that. Give the
   ledge twenty minutes at room temperature and wipe it down with alcohol.

3. **Dry-fit first, with no tape.** Sit both bars on their ledges, slide the
   drawer in and out a few times, and check two things: that it no longer scrapes,
   and that the drawer front now closes flush. If it rocks, the bottom edge has
   an elephant's foot on it — a pass with the deburring tool or thirty seconds on
   the sandpaper fixes it.

4. **Tape them down.** One strip of VHB down the centre of the flat bottom of each
   bar, press hard for thirty seconds. It holds immediately and reaches full
   strength over about a day.

5. **Put the drawer back and leave it alone overnight** before you load it with
   anything heavy.

</Steps>

## Don't glue it

VHB is deliberate. Superglue or epoxy would hold just as well and you would never
get the bar off again, and this is a part that lives inside a fridge and needs to
come out when you clean it. Tape lets you peel it, wash it and put it back.

:::caution
This is a structural part under the drawer, not a surface for food. FDM layer
lines are not cleanable in any meaningful sense — nothing should sit directly on
it, and it should come out and get scrubbed whenever the drawer does.
:::
