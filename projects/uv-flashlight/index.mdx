A handheld UV source for the bench — checking gels, spotting contamination, and
finding what a spray bottle missed. A printed body around a UV LED array and a
battery, with no microcontroller in it at all.

:::note[This one is still a write-up, not a build]
The print files for this haven't been published yet, and there is no firmware —
it is a light and a switch. What's below is the parts list and the reasoning, so
you can build the electrical side today; the printed body follows when the CAD
is cleaned up.

## Wavelength matters more than anything else

This is the part worth getting right, because most "UV" torches sold online are
the wrong one for lab work.

| Band | Wavelength | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| UV-A "blacklight" | 395–405 nm | Pet stains, counterfeit notes. **Sold as UV, barely is** |
| **UV-A** | **365 nm** | Fluorescent dyes, gel work, contamination checks |
| UV-B / UV-C | < 320 nm | Sterilisation. **Not this project** |

395 nm emitters are cheaper and look brighter to the eye — which is exactly the
problem, because that visible purple swamps the fluorescence you are trying to
see. **Buy 365 nm** and accept that it looks dimmer.

## Parts

- [365 nm UV LED array or emitter](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=365nm+UV+LED+emitter+high+power&tag=brau01-20) — not 395 nm
- [Constant-current LED driver](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=constant+current+LED+driver+module+1A&tag=brau01-20) matched to the emitter
- [18650 cell and holder](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=18650+battery+holder+with+leads&tag=brau01-20), or a USB power bank
- [Momentary or latching push switch](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=16mm+latching+push+button+switch&tag=brau01-20)
- [ZWB2 / UG-1 filter glass](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=ZWB2+UV+pass+filter+glass&tag=brau01-20) — optional, and the single biggest improvement
- A small heatsink for anything above about 3 W

:::danger
365 nm UV damages eyes and skin, and it does it without hurting at the time.
Wear [UV-blocking safety glasses](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=UV+protection+safety+glasses+400nm&tag=brau01-20) — not sunglasses — and never point it at anyone.
Cheap emitters also leak shorter wavelengths, which is another reason for the
filter.
:::

## Why the filter glass is worth it

A ZWB2 filter passes UV and blocks the visible violet that comes with it. Without
one you get a bright purple flood and washed-out fluorescence; with one the beam
looks almost dark and whatever is fluorescing lights up clearly. On a cheap build
it is the part that makes the difference between a novelty and a tool.

## Heat

UV LEDs are less efficient than white ones — most of what you put in comes out as
heat, and they dim permanently if you cook them. Bond the emitter to aluminium,
not directly to the printed body, and if you are running more than a couple of
watts print the body in **PETG** rather than PLA.
