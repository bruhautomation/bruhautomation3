# Wire Rack Shelf Brackets

Printed brackets that stiffen a sagging chrome wire shelf and tie its frame wire
back to the post, so the corner joint is located by something other than
friction.

## What's in this folder

Nothing yet — no STL, no CAD, no photos. The write-up at
[bruhautomation.com/projects/wire-rack-shelf-brackets](https://bruhautomation.com/projects/wire-rack-shelf-brackets/)
is the design brief: how the split-sleeve joint works, the four measurements any
bracket has to hit, and the material argument.

## Fit

Wire shelving is not standardised. Measure your own rack before drawing or
scaling anything:

- **Post diameter**, on the plain barrel between grooves — not in a groove
- **Groove pitch**, counted over 100 mm
- **Frame wire diameter**, the thick perimeter wire round the shelf
- **Mat wire diameter and pitch**, centre to centre across several wires

The frame wire is always fatter than the mat wires. Cutting both slots to the
frame number is the usual way to get a bracket that rattles.

## Hardware

- PETG. A bracket on a shelf is loaded every second of its life, and PLA creeps
  under a constant load at room temperature — it never cracks, it just stops
  being the shape you printed. Carbon-fibre PLA is stiffer and creeps the same
- M5 button-head bolts and nuts for a two-piece clamp, or M3 with heat-set
  inserts for the smaller brackets
- 12 in heavy-duty zip ties, as the fastener-free route and as a backstop
- Wire rack shelving, and spare split sleeves to match its post diameter

Buy the split sleeves, don't print them. A printed sleeve carries the whole
shelf on a plastic wedge, alone, permanently.

## Printing

PETG, 0.2 mm layers, five or more walls, 40% gyroid infill, no supports.

Orient so the load pulls along the extrusions rather than across the layer
lines: a C-clamp that wraps a post goes on the plate with its bore axis
vertical, so every layer is a complete closed C.
