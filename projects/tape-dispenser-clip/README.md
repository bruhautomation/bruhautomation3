# Tape Dispenser Clip

A printed bracket that fixes a bench-top lab tape dispenser somewhere your hand
already goes — the front lip of the bench, a shelf upright, the frame beside a
cabinet — so a strip comes off one-handed while the other hand is holding the
thing being labelled.

**Build guide:** [bruhautomation.com/projects/tape-dispenser-clip](https://bruhautomation.com/projects/tape-dispenser-clip/)

## Hardware

- 3D-printed bracket and clip — PETG in a lab, PLA+ on a desk. PLA crazes with
  repeated alcohol contact and goes chalky under a germicidal lamp
- One of three mounts, not all three:
  - 6 × 3 mm neodymium discs in printed pockets, for steel
  - 3M VHB, foam mounting tape or industrial hook-and-loop, for a flat surface
  - Printed jaws over a lip or rail, with TPU pads or rubber bumpers for grip
- Adhesive-backed steel plates, if the magnetic version is going onto a surface
  that isn't magnetic
- 99% isopropyl alcohol or alcohol prep pads for surface prep before any
  adhesive
- M3 heat-set inserts and stainless M3 hardware, only for a jaw that clamps with
  a screw rather than springing

## Notes

The load is the tear, not the weight. A tape dispenser weighs nothing; the pull
that takes a strip off it arrives fast and stops dead. Mount the bracket on a
vertical face so that pull runs down the surface — adhesive and magnets are both
strong in shear and weak in peel.

Test the surface with a magnet before printing the magnetic version. Lab benches
and biosafety cabinet decks are usually 304 stainless and effectively
non-magnetic, even though the painted casework next to them isn't.

Keep the dispenser's own serrated blade. A printed cutting edge crushes tape
instead of severing it, and no slicer setting fixes that.

Not autoclavable — PETG softens around 80 °C, an autoclave runs at 121 °C — and
an FDM part is porous, so it stays outside the sterile field.
